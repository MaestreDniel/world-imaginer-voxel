## Points for the user

Choices only the user could make, each changing what gets built. **Answered 2026-10-27.** They were put to the user in the order glass, water, ice, names, holding the mouse (Points 1, 5, 2, 4, 3 below); the user: "Entonces vamos con lo recomendado en todo del 1 al 4. Al leer el punto 5, por accesibilidad y/o gustos me parece interesante configurar las dos posibilidades y dejar seleccionar al usuario". So Points 1, 2, 4 and 5 take the recommendation, and Point 3 becomes a setting with both behaviours (D6).
1. **How glass looks before SP8a's textures.** *Answer: the recommendation.* Blocks are flat colours until SP8a, and a flat cutout block is either fully opaque or fully invisible. **Recommendation:** clear glass with a thin frame in its colour at each block's edges (Minecraft's glass is mostly clear with a frame); it stays in the cutout pass, as the master asks (D12). **Alternative:** tinted see-through glass (a pale blue pane you see through), which needs a new blended pass for solid blocks: about 2-3 days more. The accepted mockup drew glass's hotbar and palette swatch as a pale translucent blue (`#cfe8f088`, `hud-palette.html:16`, `:42`), which is closer to the alternative; the swatch is only the HUD's drawing of the block, but decide with it in view.
2. **An `ice` block.** *Answer: the recommendation.* The brief says you slide "on ice and packed ice", but the world has only `packed_ice` (`src/world/blocks/defs.ts:52`). **Recommendation:** add `ice` now (Terrain group, light blue, slippery, lets light through dimmed by 1 as water does); SP8b's frozen lakes will need it anyway. **Alternative:** no new block; you slide on `packed_ice` only. Without `ice`: 24 types, 32 states, ids 25 … 56, `stateCount` 57 (§1.1).
3. **Holding a mouse button.** *Answer: both, as a setting the user chooses ("configurar las dos posibilidades y dejar seleccionar al usuario"); one block per click is the default (the controller's call), D6.* **Recommendation (as asked):** one block per click; holding does not repeat, so each click is one undo step, as the brief's undo says. **Alternative:** Minecraft creative's repeat while held (break every 0.3 s, place every 0.2 s), each repeat one undo step.
4. **Names that can never change.** *Answer: the recommendation.* Every block's name is permanent once published (master §2.2), and SP8b adds 9 tree species. **Recommendation:** `oak_planks` and `oak_log` (shown as "Oak planks", "Oak log"), so birch, spruce and the others sit beside them later. **Alternative:** `planks` and `log` (the master's hotbar words), which then always mean oak.
5. **Water: how much you float.** *Answer: the recommendation.* The confirmed brief says "in water you pass through, float a little, are slowed, and swim up with Space" (`design-brief.md:19`); the master's "buoyancy 0.02, drag 0.8" (master §4.17) are Minecraft 1.8's in-water constants, under which you sink at 2 blocks/s, and the draft read "float a little" as that. **Recommendation:** today's Minecraft water: with no key you sink very slowly (0.5 blocks/s), Space rises at 3.5 blocks/s, Shift dives at 4.5 (in-water gravity 0.005 per tick in place of 0.02, §3.2). **Alternative:** real floating: with no key you rise to the surface at 0.5 blocks/s and rest there with your eyes at the water line until Shift.

## Goal

The world becomes a creative sandbox you can build in and walk through. LMB breaks the aimed block, RMB places the hotbar's block, MMB picks; a crosshair and a black outline show the aimed block within reach (6 walking, 10 flying). A 9-slot hotbar (1-9 or the wheel) starts with stone, dirt, grass, oak planks, glass, oak log, torch, water bucket and lava bucket; E opens a creative palette of every placeable block in four groups (Building, Wool, Terrain, Fluids) with a search box. 25 block types are added: oak planks, oak logs (axis by the clicked face), cobblestone, bricks, glass, torches (on the floor and on walls) that emit light, ladders, ice and 16 wools. F switches between flight and walking: a 0.6 × 1.8 body that collides, steps up 0.6, climbs ladders, swims in water and lava (still, placed by bucket) and slides on ice. A teleport while walking lands you on the ground. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo blocks when the 3D has the controls, and the map's settings when the map has them. Underneath: a sim worker that owns Published columns, an edit writer, incremental relight, the in-memory DiffStore with read-through so edits survive flying away and back and a terrain Apply, the edit remesh at priority −1000, view-free block reads for physics and the raycast. It is measured by L3 (500 random edits, incremental vs full light), E5's session part, G2's edit part (edit → visible p95 ≤ 50 ms) from the bench, a relight bench row (p95 ≤ 3 ms) and physics and palette unit tests. The user's check is building and walking. The generated world does not change (`GENERATOR_VERSION` 5, the 59 goldens byte-identical).

## Non-goals

- **Saving across a reload, worlds, export, the start menu, graphics settings, the world-modification mode and the backup copy, saved places:** SP5b (the brief). In SP5a edits live in memory for the session.
- **The selection tool** (select, copy, cut, paste, move): SP5c.
- **Flowing fluids:** SP7. Bucket water and lava stay still; breaking a lake rim drains nothing.
- **Real shapes and textures:** SP8a. Stairs, slabs, doors, trapdoors, fences and fence gates are not added (the brief: "so they do not show as cubes"); torches and ladders get provisional boxes (D12).
- **Health, damage, hunger, item counts** (the brief: creative only). Lava does not burn.
- **Sneaking** (master §5.9's "Shift (down/sneak)": slow walk and the edge guard): not in the brief; handed to SP10 (§10). In SP5a, Shift in walk mode swims down and holds on a ladder (D3, D5).
- **Key remapping** (SP4a spec :31): not in the brief; handed to SP10.
- The diff codec `ColumnDiffV1` and E1: SP5b (D20). E2-E4, E5's flush part, E6, E7: SP5b.

## Decisions taken by this spec (for the user's review)

The brief and the recon left these open. The recommendation is the text of the section cited; the alternative is stated. Each starts with what the user would notice.

### Decisions with a visible effect

- **D1 The world opens in flight; F switches** (§3.1). *You notice:* after the loading screen you fly as today; F drops you into walking where you are (you fall to the ground), F again flies from where you stand. The mode is not remembered across a reload (SP5b stores the player per world). Alternative: open walking (the browser smoke's `stream` step and the bench assume flight, `test/tools/uiSmoke.ts:72-73`), or remember the mode in `wi.world.settings`.
- **D2 Walking feels like Minecraft** (§3.2). *You notice:* walking about 4.4 blocks/s, sprinting (Ctrl) about 5.7, a jump of about 1.25 blocks, falling up to 78 blocks/s, eyes 1.62 above the feet. Physics runs at a fixed 20 Hz with Minecraft's per-tick constants (the master's terminal velocity 78 and drag 0.8 are those constants; its "buoyancy 0.02" is Minecraft 1.8's in-water gravity, which Point 5 replaces with today's 0.005) and the camera is interpolated between ticks, so it is smooth at any refresh rate. Alternative: frame-rate physics with converted constants (no interpolation; the master's per-tick figures only approximated).
- **D3 Swimming** (§3.2; Point 5). *You notice:* in water you sink very slowly (0.5 blocks/s), move at 2 blocks/s, rise with Space at 3.5 blocks/s and dive with Shift at 4.5; in lava you sink at 0.8, move at 0.8, rise at 0.8 and dive at 2.4 blocks/s; swimming against a bank lifts you out. Every speed is the distance moved per second once steady (§3.2). Alternative: Point 5's floating; or the draft's Minecraft 1.8 values (sink 2 blocks/s).
- **D4 Ice slides** (§3.2; Point 2). *You notice:* on `ice` and `packed_ice` you reach 90 % of full speed in about 1 s instead of 0.2 s and slide about 1.75 blocks after letting go (0.26 on other ground). Alternative: Point 2's (no `ice`).
- **D5 Ladders** (§3.2). *You notice:* touching a ladder, Space climbs at 2.35 blocks/s, Shift holds you still, and with neither you slide down at most 2.35 blocks/s. Alternative: the master's bare rule (Space climbs, no hold).
- **D6 Clicks** (§4.1; Point 3). *You notice:* the first click only takes the mouse, as today; after it, LMB breaks, RMB places, MMB picks, one action per click by default; the help's settings (H) gain "Hold mouse button to repeat" (off by default), and with it on a held button repeats its action every 0.3 s for breaking and 0.2 s for placing (Minecraft creative's rates), each repeat aimed afresh and each one undo step; MMB never repeats; the choice is remembered in this browser (`wi.world.settings`) until SP5b's settings screen takes it. The right-click menu never opens over the world. A block is never placed where it would overlap your body, walking or flying (in flight your body is the same 0.6 × 1.8 box under the eye). Both behaviours are the user's answer to Point 3; the default and the place of the setting are the controller's call. Alternative: on by default; a key to toggle it; no body in flight.
- **D7 What a click does** (§4.2). *You notice:* blocks go on the face you aim at; a torch on a floor stands, on a wall hangs, on a ceiling is refused; a ladder on a wall hangs on it, facing away from the wall, and on a floor or ceiling faces you; a log lies along the clicked face's axis; a torch is refused inside water or lava; buckets fill the voxel in front of the face, the empty bucket removes the first water or lava on the ray; MMB selects the slot that already holds that block, or puts it in the active slot. The brief's "a ladder by where you look" holds whenever you face the wall you click; a glancing click on a side face still hangs the ladder on that face, because a ladder must hang on a wall (master §4.12: "from the player's yaw (and the clicked face)"). Alternative: the master's rules only, without the fluid refusals (a torch inside water would sit in the water); a ladder always facing away from the look (it may then hang on no wall).
- **D8 Aiming** (§3.5). *You notice:* a white `+` in the centre (shown from the first click on) and a thin black outline around the aimed block (around the stick of a torch, the plate of a ladder); a block under water shows its outline through the water's tint; reach 6 blocks walking, 10 flying, through water and lava. Alternative: the outline of the whole cell for torches and ladders (aims at empty air around them); the outline drawn with no depth test (then also seen through terrain).
- **D9 The hotbar and the wheel** (§4.3). *You notice:* nine 40 px slots 10 px above the bottom centre, the active one framed in white; the block's name for 1.5 s above it on a change; the wheel and 1-9 change the slot, walking or flying; Ctrl+wheel and `+`/`-` change the flying speed while flying, and while walking Ctrl+wheel changes the slot like the wheel (Ctrl is sprint, so a sprinting player can still scroll); the world's notices ("Could not teleport", the undo notice) move up above the bar. Ctrl+1 … Ctrl+8 switch Chrome's tabs and a page cannot stop that, so the help says to let go of Ctrl before a digit. The hotbar is remembered in this browser across reloads until SP5b keeps it per world. Alternative: the wheel changes speed while flying and the slot only while walking (two meanings for one control).
- **D10 The palette** (§4.4). *You notice:* E releases the mouse and shows a panel over the world: a search box (not focused until you click it, so E closes the panel), then Building (oak planks, oak log, cobblestone, bricks, glass, torch, ladder), Wool (16, Minecraft's colour order), Terrain (stone, bedrock, ice and the 22 terrain blocks) and Fluids (water bucket, lava bucket, empty bucket); the world pauses behind it; a click on a block puts it in the active slot; hovering names it below the grid; E (outside the search box) closes it and takes the mouse back, Esc closes it, M or H close it and open the map or the help. Every block except air and the wall torch is placeable, bedrock included (a creative sandbox). Alternative: bedrock not placeable; the search box focused at opening (then only Esc closes).
- **D11 Block names** (§1.1; Point 4). *You notice:* the palette's names. Alternative: Point 4's.
- **D12 How the new blocks look until SP8a** (§1.3; Point 1). *You notice:* flat colours as today; glass clear with a thin frame (Point 1); a torch is a thin upright stick, a wall torch a stick against its wall, a ladder a thin plate on its wall; a log's two end faces are lighter than its bark, so its axis shows; bucket lava is an opaque bright orange block lighting its surroundings at 15; ice is a pale blue block; where glass or ice meets water you see the water's surface (an aquarium wall looks into tinted water), never two faces flickering. Alternative: torch and ladder as full cubes (the master's "until SP8a box-model states use SHAPE cube", which also traps a torch's light, §1.2); logs with no visible axis.
- **D13 Undo and redo by who has the controls** (§4.5). *You notice:* with the 3D in control, Ctrl+Z undoes your last block change (a break, a place, a bucket) and Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) redoes it, with a short "Undo · blocks" or "Redo · blocks" note; with the map in control, they act on the map's settings as today. Each history keeps 100 steps for the session; a new block change clears the block redo steps. An undo whose place cannot be edited at that moment is refused with "Can't undo there: not loaded" or "Can't undo there: regenerating" (stale after a terrain change), and stays in the history; an undo pressed while a block change is still being written waits for it. Alternative: no note (the change is in view); an unloaded undo applied to the stored edits and shown when you return.
- **D14 Edits survive flying away and a terrain change** (§2.5, §2.7). *You notice:* fly 10,000 blocks away and back, and your build is there; change the terrain from the map, and your blocks stay at the same coordinates on the new terrain (floating or buried). That is the brief's SP5b rule (`design-brief.md:33`, a controller's call), applied here one SP early. Until SP5b's modification mode and backup copy, a terrain change can bury a build, so in SP5a the brief's aim that "a world with builds must not be destroyed by" terrain experiments (`design-brief.md:12`) holds only in that the blocks are kept. The map's edits stay live in SP5a. Alternative: hold the map's Apply in a world with edits until SP5b (a new SP5a-only rule the user would have to learn and unlearn).
- **D15 Teleport while walking** (§3.4). *You notice:* you arrive standing on the ground at the clicked point, looking the same way; over the sea you arrive at the surface and then sink slowly (D3); for the moment before the terrain there is built you hover, then you are set on the ground. Alternative: arrive in the air and fall (24 blocks of fall at every walking teleport).
- **D16 The small additions you will see** (§4.6). These are the spec's own, not the brief's: the HUD's position line starts with "Walk" or "Fly" (the mockup's readout, `hud-palette.html:8`); the HUD's speed line reads "walking" while walking; the HUD's "edit p95 n ms" line once you have edited (master §4.12: "measured in the HUD"); the undo notes of D13; the notices "Editing stopped: the edit worker failed; this session's edits are lost" and "Could not edit: memory full" (§2.1, §2.2); the help's new Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y line and its note on Ctrl before a digit, and the loading tips that name the new keys (§4.5, §4.6); Shift swims down and holds on ladders (D3, D5); the hop out of water (D3); the log end colour and the glass frame (D12); the lift out of blocks (D29); the setting's wording "Hold mouse button to repeat" (D6). Alternative: none of them.
- **D27 The split in the master and the code, and when caves come** (§8, §9). *You notice:* caves (SP6) start after the selection tool (SP5c): the order of work is SP5a → SP5b → SP5c → SP6. The brief's "in this order" orders the three SP5 parts and does not say where caves go. `SubProjectId` gains 'SP5a', 'SP5b', 'SP5c' in place of 'SP5'; `SP_DEPS`: SP5a ← SP4b, SP4c; SP5b ← SP5a; SP5c ← SP5b (its clipboard survives reloads); SP6 ← SP3c, SP5c; SP7 ← SP6, SP5a; SP8c ← SP7, SP5a; the critical path runs SP4c → SP5a → SP5b → SP5c → SP6. Sizes: SP5a L, SP5b M and SP5c M, provisional until their specs. Alternative: SP6 after SP5a only, SP5b and SP5c in parallel (caves could start before saved worlds).
- **D29 Lifted out of blocks** (§3.2). *You notice:* if a block ends up inside your body while walking (the terrain regenerates around you after a map change, a column loads around you, or a block you placed lands where you have just moved), you are lifted straight up to the first space where you fit, on that tick. Alternative: the master's rule only (the scan at F and after a teleport); a body inside blocks then cannot move at all under the per-axis sweep.

### Technical decisions (no visible effect)

- **D17 One sim worker owns Published final sets** (§2.1). A module worker attached as thread 15 (`src/world/store/reclaim.ts:10`, `:32`), outside the pool: it receives no `configure` and no world jobs. The main thread decides which columns may be edited (only it knows stage and epoch) and pins them; the sim validates, writes, relights, records the diff and replies. If the sim fails, the session's edits are gone (the DiffStore lives in the sim, §2.4): every wait on it resolves with no diff, every pin it held is released, and a failure inside a pool lock is fatal, as for any worker (§2.1). Alternative: edits on the main thread (blocks rendering during relight; the master puts them in the sim worker, §4.1).
- **D18 An edit writer for Published columns, always copy-on-write** (§2.2). `reopenPublished(cx, cz)`, beside `reopenColumn` (`src/world/store/store.ts:512-517`, which refuses status 3), replaces whole sections through `storeChannel`'s copy-on-write (`src/world/store/section.ts:73-87`) and bumps versions without changing the status. Final blocks and fluid share the proto's slots (`store.ts:386-399`), so an in-place write would change the proto. Alternative: an in-place voxel write (corrupts the proto; races readers).
- **D19 Relight on a sparse section cache** (§2.3). `light/relight.ts` runs the removal and re-add BFS and the sky re-seed over an injected accessor that reads the 3 × 3's sections in place and copies a section's light only when the BFS first writes it. Alternative: copy SP4a's 46 × 384 × 46 region per edit (about 1-2 ms of copy before any BFS, master §4.8, against the 3 ms budget).
- **D20 DiffStore entries hold the edited value only; the generated value is read from the retained proto** (§2.4). An edit whose value equals the column's current generated value removes the entry. The page keeps every proto (`retainAllProtos: true`, `src/ui/world/worldPage.ts:1225`; the coordinator then never queues a proto free, `src/engine/coordinator.ts:811`), and in SP5a D adds nothing to the proto's blocks and fluid (`src/gen/pipeline/decorateStage.ts:69-70`), so the proto is the generated value under the current terrain, also after an Apply. An edit on a column with no proto is refused (`'noProto'`, §2.1), which cannot happen while `retainAllProtos` is on. The read-through transfers plain typed arrays; the codec and E1 are SP5b's. Alternative: master §2.7's remembered generated value (stale after an Apply; it would need a per-genKey rule).
- **D21 Read-through in the page's D adapter** (§2.5). Before a D for a column with entries, the adapter asks the sim for its diff and passes it in the job; the coordinator is unchanged. Alternative: a wait state in the coordinator (more code in the scheduler for a wait of about a millisecond).
- **D22 Edit remeshes are issued at once and run first** (§2.6). The `edited` reply hands the dirty columns to `coordinator.markEdited`, which issues their MESHes at once from an edit queue, outside the stream heaps, past the coordinator's `total` and `inFlight[3]` caps and its generation gate (`coordinator.ts:957`, `:961`, `:968`; a MESH reads only final sets, never params). The pool takes them as `meshColumn(cx, cz, {edit: true})` on the aux lane at priority −1000, which a free worker takes first (`src/engine/workerPool.ts:417-419`), uncapped by `auxCap`; `cancelWorld` also removes them there. The coordinator's per-slot `editSeq` lets a meshed column be meshed again and staged again in the same epoch. Alternative: mesh on the sim worker (no wait for a free worker, but meshing serialised behind edits and relight); or the edit MESH through heap 3 under the caps (it then waits behind streaming, which would be most of G2's 50 ms).
- **D23 The block-light scan only where an emitter can be** (§1.2). A torch makes `tableEmits` always true (`src/light/initial.ts:237`), so every L job would scan 812,544 voxels. The column record's reserved `REC_DIFF_FLAG` (`src/world/store/columnTable.ts:40`) marks a column that holds or held an edit: D sets it when it overlays a diff and the sim when it writes an entry (so a neighbour regenerated beside a live torch still scans), both with `Atomics.store`; it is cleared only when the record is claimed again (`clearRecord`, `columnTable.ts:136`). L scans only when a column of its 3 × 3 has the flag or the region holds lava. Alternative: measure the always-on scan (the `light.column` bench's p50 4 ms and p99 8 ms gates, `test/bench/gates.ts:25-26`, against 2.15 ms today); or a flag only on columns holding an emitting entry (one more rule that must stay exact).
- **D24 STORE-3 fixed now** (§2.1). The reclamation epoch is compared wrap-aware, as SP4b §12.2 prescribes, in both places that compare it: `drain` takes the oldest live reader relative to G (the largest `(G − e) | 0`; today it takes a plain numeric minimum starting from `Infinity`, `src/world/store/reclaim.ts:128-133`) and frees an entry while `((tag − m) | 0) < 0`, with "no live reader" kept as its own case that frees everything; `enterRead` skips 0 both when it loads G and when it announces. The sim worker retires a slot on every edit and relight. Alternative: show the release rate stays unreachable (an argument, not a test); or widen the counter (SP4b §12.2's other option).

## 2. The edit pipeline

### 2.1 The sim worker (D17, D24)

- `workers/sim.worker.ts` wires the handler `workers/simHandler.ts` (`createSimHandler`: the message guard and the dispatch, as `task.worker.ts` wires `taskHandler.ts`) over the pure core `sim/edits.ts` (`applyEdits`, `readDiff`); the guard lives in `workers/`, since `sim/` value-imports only the L2 core layers, not `workers/protocol.ts`. Only `engine/` spawns workers, so `engine/simClient.ts` (`createBrowserSim`, `createSimClient`) spawns the sim and owns the entry set and the `diff` waits; the world page calls it after the pool (`worldPage.ts:1159-1172`) and attaches the sim as thread 15 to `store.handles()` (`AttachMsg`, `src/workers/protocol.ts:122`).
- **On a sim failure** (an error event, or a reply the page's guard refuses): the page marks thread 15 dead (`reclaim.ts:186-190`), disables editing and shows "Editing stopped: the edit worker failed; this session's edits are lost". The DiffStore lived in the sim (§2.4), so the page resolves every pending and later `diff` wait of §2.5 with "no diff" and empties its entry set (D then runs without an overlay, and a column regenerated later shows the generated terrain), and releases every `simPins` count (`coordinator.simUnpinAll`, each slot then as `simUnpin`, §2.7). A sim that dies inside a pool's allocation lock turns that lock into `StoreCorrupt` (SP4b's dead-owner rule, `src/world/store/pool.ts:15`), which is fatal as for any worker (`src/engine/coordinator.ts:1155-1157`); otherwise the world keeps streaming.
- It wraps its store reads and writes in its own read sections (SP4b §12.2), and drains its limbo after each batch and every 100 ms while the limbo is not empty (it has no reply loop like the pool's, `task.worker.ts:12`).
- **Messages:** main → sim `{type: 'edits', id, edits: [{x, y, z, op: 'set' | 'revert', value, expect}]}` (`value` = state << 8 | fluid; `revert` removes the entry and writes the generated value; `expect` is the voxel's value the main thread saw when it decided, §4.1); sim → main `{type: 'edited', id, prev: Uint32Array (the entry before each edit, or 0xFFFFFFFF for none), next: Uint32Array (the entry after each edit, 0xFFFFFFFF when the edit removed it or left none; as long as `prev`), dirty: column keys to remesh (§2.6), entryCols: column keys holding entries, refused: null | 'unloaded' | 'changed' | 'noProto' | 'full', relightMs, editMs}` (the block history records `prev` and `next` as a step's two sides, §4.5); main → sim `{type: 'diff', id, cx, cz}` → `{type: 'diff', id, cx, cz, sy: Uint8Array, idx: Uint16Array, value: Uint32Array}` (transferred; `id` matches the request; zero-length arrays when the column holds no entries, which the client reads as no diff). The sim handles messages in order. A batch is all or nothing: every voxel is validated (its column at status 3, its value still `expect`, a proto to read) before the first write, and any refusal refuses the whole batch; `full` rolls back the partial writes (§2.2).
- **The gate (main thread):** `coordinator.editState(cx, cz): 0 | 1 | 2` is 0 (open) when every column of the edited column's 3 × 3 is tracked, Published, at the applied epoch (`colEpoch === wEpoch`) and not failed; 2 ("regenerating") when every column is tracked, not failed and Published or stale, and one is stale (`colEpoch ≠ wEpoch`, after an Apply); 1 ("not loaded") otherwise. The page and the block history word their refusals from it (D13). An in-flight MESH does not close the gate: the writer is copy-on-write and the MESH's result is dropped by its versions (`coordinator.ts:1094-1105`); on a Published column `busy` is otherwise only `T(protoOnly)` (`coordinator.ts:400-401`), which `retainAllProtos` rules out. The page posts an edit only at 0, and adds 1 to `simPins` of the 3 × 3 (`coordinator.simPin`) until the reply (`simUnpin`, §2.7); `freeable` (`src/engine/coordinator.ts:834-837`) also requires `simPins` 0. Since the 3 × 3 is Published, no L in flight reads the edited column, so an L is never dropped by an edit (a dropped L cannot recover, `src/world/store/store.ts:514-515`). The gate is how master §8 risk 7's "edits-during-light" is met: an edit never runs beside an L that reads it, so L3 checks edits on a lit region instead of fuzzing that race (§9 rewords the risk).
- **STORE-3:** `reclaim.ts`'s `drain` and `enterRead` as D24.

### 2.2 Validate, write, heightmaps (D18)

For each batch, in the sim, inside a read section: validate every voxel (§2.1); then, for each edit: open `reopenPublished(cx, cz)` (stale guard as `store.ts:311-320`); read the section's blocks and fluid into scratch; set the voxel; replace that section's blocks and fluid only, through `replaceBlocks(sy, blocks, fluid)` (copy-on-write through `storeChannel`, `src/world/store/section.ts:73-96`, meta recomputed as `store.ts:354-357`; unlike `setFinal`, which marks the light written, `store.ts:380`, it sets `wroteBlocks` only), a method of `ColumnWriter` as well as of the edit writer, since D's overlay writes through D's own writer, a `ColumnWriter` reopened at Proto (`src/workers/worldJobs.ts:39`), and `gen/` sees only `src/world/store/api.ts`'s types (§2.5); set `REC_DIFF_FLAG` with `Atomics.store`, through `setDiffFlag()`, likewise a method of both writers (D23); `patchHeightmaps(blockAt, fluidAt, aux, lx, lz, fromY)` rescans that block column of the four heightmaps from `fromY` down with the same predicates as `finalHeightmaps.ts:34-65`, where `fromY` = min(319, max(the edited y, each of the four old maps − 1)) (the old maps hold y + 1, so the scan starts at the higher of the edit and the old tops; a rollback rescans from 319), in place in aux A as D writes it (the one in-place write on the edit path: element-wise Int16 stores, which `heightAt`'s readers tolerate, `store.ts:599-608`); relight (§2.3); write back each dirty light section by copy-on-write (no light-once refusal on this writer, `src/world/store/api.ts:57-63`); `publish()` bumps `blockVersion` of the edited column and `lightVersion` of every column whose light changed, without a status change. `StoreFull` refuses the batch (`refused: 'full'`, notice "Could not edit: memory full") after undoing its partial writes.

### 2.3 Incremental relight (D19)

`light/relight.ts`, pure over `RelightAccess` (`block(i)`, `fluid(i)`, `light(i)`, `setLight(i, v)` over the 3 × 3's local indices, voxel `i = (row · 48 + z) · 48 + x` with x and z 0 … 47 and row = y + 64, and a fifth method `lightBlocking(x, z)` returning that block column's `LIGHT_BLOCKING` as a row 0 … 384; `light/` imports only types from `world/store/api.ts`, SP0 spec :152-169):
- per channel, the removal BFS from the edited voxel (a ring of (index, old level)), then the re-add BFS from the removal frontier and from new sources (a placed torch or lava; an opened voxel takes light from its neighbours);
- sky: where the edit changes `fallBlocked` (`light.ts:53-55`) at the top of the light column, the straight fall is re-seeded from the new `LIGHT_BLOCKING` down to the old one (opening a shaft lights its full height at 15; closing it removes it), then the same removal and re-add;
- each step uses `propagate`'s rule (`initial.ts:83-113`): never across a full face, cost max(1, effective opacity); a fluid voxel's effective opacity is 1 (`light.ts:38-41`);
- it touches at most the 3 × 3 (master §4.8) and returns, per column, the dirty light sections and which of its four borders and four corners hold a voxel whose light changed (the remesh set, §2.6).
The result equals a full recompute (L3, §6.1).

### 2.4 The DiffStore (D20)

`sim/diffStore.ts`: `Map<colKey, Map<sy, Map<voxelIdx, u32>>>` of absolute final values (master §2.7). A `set` whose value equals the generated value (the proto's block and fluid at that voxel) removes the entry; `revert` always removes it. The store never drops entries on its own: after an Apply, entries stay at their coordinates (D14). `entryCols` in each reply keeps the main thread's set of columns with entries. An edit on a column with no proto (`store.proto` null, `src/world/store/store.ts:543-546`) is refused (`'noProto'`); the sim asserts this in tests, where E5 and the integration test build the coordinator with `retainAllProtos: true` as the page does.

### 2.5 Read-through and D's overlay (D21)

- The page's `worldJobsOf` adapter (`coordinator.ts:314-335`): for a D on a column in the entry set, it awaits the sim's `diff` and passes it as `diff` on the `decorate` message (`WorldJobMsg`, `protocol.ts:130`, `:418-419`, `:452` gain the field and its validation). The pool posts every job without a transfer list (`workerPool.ts:434`), so the decorate message's diff is cloned, not transferred (a few KB); only the sim's `diff` reply is transferred (§2.1). A column with an unanswered edit is pinned (§2.1), so it is never freed and regenerated before the sim recorded its entry (the E5 race). After a sim failure the wait resolves at once with no diff (§2.1).
- `decorateStage` (`decorateStage.ts:11`, `:68-75`): with a diff, after `shareFinal`, each section with entries is rebuilt from the proto plus the entries and replaced with D's writer's `replaceBlocks` (`ColumnWriter`, §2.2; copy-on-write, so the proto stays generated, and no light written, so no `lightVersion` bump); the heightmaps are computed from the final set, reading the rebuilt sections from D's scratch, since `nb.final` is null below Decorated (`store.ts:123-124`); `REC_DIFF_FLAG` is set through the writer's `setDiffFlag()`. Without a diff the path is byte-identical to today's (no extra writes or version bumps), so the goldens and every metric stay.
- A column regenerated after an Apply or a reload therefore shows its edits on its terrain (D14). An edit is refused on a stale column (§2.1).

### 2.6 The edit remesh (D22)

- **The dirty set:** the reply's `dirty` lists every column whose padded view holds a voxel whose block, fluid or light changed: for each such voxel, its own column and each face or diagonal neighbour whose one-voxel ring includes it (the ring covers all eight neighbours, `src/world/store/padded.ts:164-191`, and AO and smooth light read the edge and corner voxels, `src/mesh/greedy.ts:9-12`). Relight reports the borders and corners per column (§2.3), so a column whose own light is unchanged but whose neighbour's border light changed is dirty, and a change at a column corner reaches the diagonal column.
- **Issue:** the reply handler calls `coordinator.markEdited(cols)` at once, not at the next frame's `update` (`frameStep.ts:129-130`): each slot's `editSeq` and `meshSeq` are bumped and the slot joins an edit queue (a preallocated ring). `pump` drains the edit queue before the stream heaps: a slot whose 3 × 3 is still Published and current and that is not busy gets its MESH at once, past `total >= N`, `inFlight[3] >= N` and the generation gate (`coordinator.ts:957`, `:961`, `:968`), counted in `inFlight[3]` and `total` so the stream jobs yield; a busy slot stays queued until its job settles. The bumped `meshSeq` drops an in-flight MESH of that slot at intake (`coordinator.ts:1113`), which requeues it through the edit queue; the bench counts these as `editDropped`, apart from `dropped`. The two "already meshed" rules (`coordinator.ts:508`, `:519-523`) do not apply to a slot whose `editSeq` is newer than its staged one; the staging assertion (`coordinator.ts:1121-1123`) allows a restage at the same epoch when the payload carries a newer `editSeq` (the renderers replace a pending payload and keep the shown one, `src/render/regionGrid.ts:397-425`).

## 3. The player

### 3.1 Modes (D1)

`FlyState` (`src/player/fly.ts:17`) gains `mode: 'fly' | 'walk'`; `y` stays the eye in both modes (the HUD, the URL and the teleport keep one convention). While walking, `walk.ts` keeps the previous and the current tick's feet, and each frame writes the drawn (interpolated) eye into `fly.x`, `fly.y` and `fly.z`, which the HUD, the URL and the camera read. ### 3.2 Walking (D2-D5, D25)

`player/walk.ts` (the controller) over `player/physics.ts` (the swept AABB), a fixed tick of 0.05 s with an accumulator (at most 5 ticks per frame, as `MAX_STEP_S` 0.25 bounds the frame, `src/ui/world/frameStep.ts:43`); the eye drawn is the previous and current tick's feet interpolated by the accumulator, plus 1.62. Velocities are in blocks per tick.

| case | per tick |
|---|---|
| ground, horizontal | v += a · wish; move; v ·= f, with f = slip · 0.91, slip 0.6 (0.98 when the block under the body's centre at feet − 0.5 is `SLIPPERY`), a = 0.1 · 0.16277136 / f³ walking, × 1.3 sprinting (Ctrl): about 4.4 and 5.7 blocks/s; on ice full speed in about 20 ticks and a slide of about 1.75 blocks |
| air, horizontal | v += 0.02 · wish (0.026 sprinting); move; v ·= 0.91 |
| vertical, out of fluid | Space on the ground: vy = 0.42 (a jump of about 1.25 blocks); move; vy = (vy − 0.08) · 0.98, terminal 3.92 (78.4 blocks/s, the master's 78) |
| water (the body overlaps a water voxel) | Space: vy += 0.04; Shift: vy −= 0.04; v += 0.02 · wish; move; v ·= 0.8 on all axes; vy −= 0.005 (Point 5; its alternative: vy += 0.005 while the eye is in water, −= 0.005 while it is not). Moved per tick once steady: vertical 5 · (input − 0.005), horizontal 5 · 0.02: sinks 0.025 (0.5 blocks/s), rises 0.175 with Space (3.5), dives 0.225 with Shift (4.5), moves 0.1 (2) |
| lava | Space +0.04, Shift −0.04, v += 0.02 · wish; move; v ·= 0.5; vy −= 0.02. Moved per tick once steady: vertical 2 · (input − 0.02), horizontal 2 · 0.02: sinks 0.04 (0.8 blocks/s), rises 0.04 (0.8), dives 0.12 (2.4), moves 0.04 (0.8) |
| bank hop | in a fluid, a horizontal move blocked and the body free 0.6 higher: vy = 0.3 |
| ladder (the body overlaps a `CLIMBABLE` voxel) | vy clamped to ±0.1175 (±2.35 blocks/s, master §4.17); Space: vy = +0.1175; Shift: vy = 0; applied after the water or lava row when both apply (a waterlogged ladder) |
| unloaded | a column under the body's footprint not Published: vy = 0 and no vertical move; horizontal moves go on and do not collide with that column's voxels (master §4.17: an unloaded column does not count as solid); a body found inside blocks once the column is Published is lifted by the unstuck rule |

- **Collision:** 0.6 × 1.8 box at the feet; per axis (y, then x, then z) in substeps of at most 0.35 blocks; `COLLIDE` cube and boxes (as cube until SP8a, master §2.2) collide, `none` and fluids never. **Step-up 0.6:** a horizontal move blocked while on the ground is retried 0.6 higher and the body then lowered onto what it finds. It cannot be seen in SP5a: every SP5a block that collides is a full cube (`src/world/blocks/defs.ts:22-58`, §1.1's table), so only the unit test's synthetic 0.5 ledge shows it until SP8a's slabs and stairs. **Unstuck** (D29): at the start of every tick, when the body intersects a colliding block (after F, after a walking teleport's snap, after a column under it is Published with other terrain, after an edit lands in it), the feet move up one block at a time until free, at most to y 400 (`frameStep.ts:49`). The test reads the body's at most 2 × 3 × 2 voxels, with no allocation.
- **Reads:** `store.blockAt`, `fluidAt` and `publishedAt` (−1, 0, false when the column is not Published; for a Published column, bedrock below y −64 and air above y 319, D25), view-free, inside one `enterRead`/`exitRead` around the walk step (the frame step reads the eye light the same way, `frameStep.ts:147-152`). The frame step's motion call (`frameStep.ts:125`) becomes a dispatch by mode.
