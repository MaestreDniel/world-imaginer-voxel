## Points decided by the user

Choices only the user can make, each with the recommendation and the alternative. **Answered by the user on 2026-10-25: the recommendation in all four** (1: no fog change; 2: the Iris Xe Firefox run required, the RTX optional; 3: a plain click teleports except while the cut tool is armed; 4: the 3D surface). The alternatives are kept below as the record of what was weighed.
1. **What you see right after a teleport.** The brief says "the world is empty for a moment, then terrain appears around the camera nearest-first". Today's fog does not react to a jump, so with the render distance at 12 you see the empty ground and the ring of new terrain growing around you, out to the fog's usual distance (176 blocks). **Recommendation:** the brief as written: no fog change at a teleport, so what arrives first is in plain view. **Alternative:** at a teleport the fog closes in around you and opens out as the terrain fills in (as it does today after you raise the render distance), so you never see the empty edge; it takes about 5.5 s to open fully at render distance 12, so the first seconds look like flying into thick fog; a faster opening (about 1.8 s) is a variant. The night fog (your SP4b note) is a separate matter and stays with SP11 (D25).
2. **Will you run the bench page in Firefox before SP4c closes?** The Chrome runs are done by an agent on your laptop, as in SP4b. Firefox needs you: open `?bench=medium` in Firefox, let it run (about 3 minutes; it now ends with an edit, its undo and a teleport) and send the downloaded JSON. **Recommendation:** the run on the Iris Xe is required at the exit (it must stay smooth: 95 % of frames within 25 ms and none over 33 ms during the edit, the undo and the teleport); the run on the RTX 3060 Mobile is optional and recorded if you send it. If a required run misses, the agent tunes the renderer and asks you for one more run. **Alternative:** both runs required, as in SP4b; or none, the Firefox side left to your own check in the browser.
3. **What a plain click on the map does in the world** (D14). The brief says "a click on the map teleports there at once". On `?map` a plain click today also does three other things: places the cut line's points (when the cut tool is armed), sets the centre of the Density tab's horizontal slice, and sets the pin (or opens the Cross-section tab near the cut line). **Recommendation:** in the world's map a plain click always teleports, except while the cut tool is armed (you arm it with its button and it disarms after its second point); the slice centre, the pin and opening the Cross-section move to Shift+click; `?map` keeps today's clicks. **Alternative:** a plain click always teleports, and the cut tool's points also need Shift+click (no exception at all, but the cut tool works differently in the world and on `?map`).
4. **The arrival height is taken from the 3D terrain, not from the map's estimate** (D12). The brief says the height "comes from the map's surface estimate". That estimate ignores the jagged peaks and overhangs the 3D terrain adds, so on a jagged mountain with the default settings you could arrive up to about 30 blocks inside the rock (up to 57 with the amplified profile), against "a little above the terrain". **Recommendation:** the landing asks the workers for the 3D surface at that point (still known before the terrain is built, and still within a frame or two), so you always arrive about 24 blocks above the real ground or water. **Alternative:** the map's estimate, as the brief worded it (sometimes arriving inside a mountain, and flying out).

## Goal

The map moves into the world. M opens the same map tool as `?map` over the 3D world, centred on the camera with an arrow for the camera's position and heading; a click teleports there at once, and terrain appears around the camera nearest-first with no loading screen. Everything `?map` edits (parameters with climate, splines, the biome table with its climate limits, presets, the seed and profile, SP3d's density- and surface-rule editors) applies to the 3D world live: the old world stays visible while the new one replaces it column by column, nearest first; a slider drag applies only its last value; a change that leaves the world as it is rebuilds nothing; undo and redo apply live too. The user edits in one of two modes, remembered: A, full screen with "Watch live" ("Ver en vivo"), or B, side by side, flying in the right half while the map's arrow follows. Underneath: one worker pool for map tiles and world jobs, the per-column epoch, `engine/invalidation.ts`, the coordinator's Apply, both section renderers' swap of a revealed column, and a pump that does not starve uploads after a teleport. It is measured by R7's Apply part (a deterministic Node metric), the scheduler test "old meshes kept on Apply", an Apply integration test and new live legs of `?bench=medium`. The user's check is some teleports and live editing in both modes, with climate experiments among them. The generated world does not change (`GENERATOR_VERSION` 5, the 59 goldens byte-identical).

## Non-goals

- **Saved places and saved worlds: SP5.** The user's model is one map per saved world ("mundo 1 tiene mapa 1"), independent of the seed; SP5 gives the map a saved-places section (open the map, go to that section, pick one, teleport). SP4c's map knows only the running world.
- **Walk mode and its teleport: SP5.** The rule is recorded for SP5 (§3.5): just above the terrain, the look direction kept.
- **A seed or profile change behaving differently from a setting: SP5** (with saved worlds, the brief). In SP4c both regenerate live like any setting.
- **The night fog: SP11** (master §10's SP11 entry, "may be pulled earlier as a small tuning"). Not pulled (D25). Point 1's teleport fog is the only fog question here.
- Apply for the decorate, light, remesh and live scopes (no such leaf exists: every schema leaf is climate- or terrain-scope, `core/params/schema.ts:8-11`): SP8b (decorate), SP10 (all scopes, badges, per-stage `stale/total` progress, G2's Apply gate, U1's counters), SP11 (live graphics).
- The master's "explicit Apply button" and "n unapplied changes" header (master §5.1): SP4c applies automatically (D4); the header with per-stage progress is SP10's.
- The teleport snap on the map, the map's spawn and player layers beyond the arrow: SP10. The master's map "bookmarks" (§5.6 "bookmarks per save", SP10's "complete map layers (… bookmarks …)") are the user's saved places, SP5's (above); §12 amends both master lines so they have one home.
- New bench rows (`npm run bench`), light or mesh goldens, any generator change.

## Decisions confirmed by the user (2026-10-25)

From the brief, played back and confirmed ("Todo correcto hasta aquí", "Sí", "Todo correcto, sigue adelante"); items marked "controller's call" were proposed and not objected to.
1. **Why:** the user will experiment a lot, though not only, with the climate settings (continentalness, erosion, temperature, …) and the biome table's climate limits, watching how the world reacts, to tune landscapes that are natural in some biomes and fantastical in others; it is judged by eye. The map is also for seeing where you are and finding a striking place to teleport to.
2. **M opens the map over the world**, centred on the camera, with an arrow for the position and the view heading (controller's call); pan and zoom as in `?map`.
3. **A click teleports at once**, with no confirmation (controller's call); in mode A the map closes, in mode B it stays open.
4. **Saved places are SP5's**, with saved worlds; one map per saved world ("mundo 1 tiene mapa 1"), independent of the seed.
5. **Teleport is instant**, with no loading screen (only the first entry into a world has one): the world is empty for a moment, then terrain appears around the camera nearest-first; the area left behind unloads.
6. **Arrival in flight:** a little above the terrain at that point, about 24 blocks above the surface, or above the water over the sea (controller's call), facing the same yaw and pitch; the height comes from the map's surface estimate (master §3.7, shared by the map, spawn and teleport), so it is known before the terrain exists. (This spec takes the height from the 3D surface estimate instead, so the arrival is never inside a jagged peak: D12, Point 4.)
7. **Walk mode (SP5):** just above the terrain, the look direction kept; recorded for SP5.
8. **Live editing covers everything `?map` edits:** parameters (climate included), splines, the biome table with its climate limits, presets, and SP3d's density- and surface-rule editors (controller's call).
9. **Seed and profile changes behave like any setting** ("Actualmente, lo mismo que con un ajuste"): the world regenerates live where the camera is, with no loading screen; SP5 may decide otherwise with saved worlds.
10. **On a change:** the old world stays visible while the new one replaces it nearest-first; a burst (a slider drag) applies only the last value; a change that does not affect the terrain rebuilds nothing; the camera does not move; if new terrain rises above the camera, the camera simply flies inside it (controller's call).
11. **Undo and redo (Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y) apply live**, to compare before and after within a couple of seconds ("deshacer y rehacer en vivo me sirve").
12. **Two modes, chosen by the user:** **A, full screen:** the map hides the 3D, with a "Ver en vivo" (watch live) toggle: on, a change hides the map and shows the 3D regenerating, and M returns to the map; off, the change applies behind and the user returns with M or ✕. **B, side by side:** the map and its panels on the left half, the 3D on the right; a click on the 3D half takes the controls (pointer lock and WASD flight, as when entering the world now); Esc gives them back to the map with the map still open; the map's arrow follows the camera while flying (the user: "Me parece interesante poder volar en 3D sin cerrar el mapa"; the proposal: "Sí"). The mode is chosen with a switch in the map's header and remembered (controller's call).
13. **The standalone `?map` stays**, with its shareable URL; the map in the world is the same tool (controller's call).
14. **Success:** the user's check, some teleports and live editing in both modes with climate experiments among them; plus the master's automatic criteria (R7's Apply part, the scheduler test "old meshes kept on Apply", as this spec sets them); the generated world unchanged (goldens, `GENERATOR_VERSION` 5).
15. **From SP4b's close:** the night fog should hide the streaming reveal as the day fog does; handed to SP11, which may pull it earlier; not SP4c's unless this spec pulls it (it does not, D25).

## Decisions taken by this spec (for the user's review)

The brief and the recon left these open. The recommendation is the text of the section cited; the alternative is stated. Each starts with what the user would notice; the ones with no visible effect are grouped at the end.

### Decisions with a visible effect

- **D2 Mounted once, lazily, then hidden and shown** (§1.3). *You notice:* the map reopens instantly, with its tiles, open sections and cut line as you left them. The map's code is preloaded 2 s after the reveal; the first M mounts the tool into its overlay host (the configure driver replays its current preview source into it, §4.2); M and ✕ hide and show it; the world page's teardown disposes it. Every disposer exists, and the browser smoke proves a mount and dispose leaves no listener or timer behind (§8.6). Alternative: mount and dispose on every M (the tile cache, the open sections and the cut line lost at each opening, and the full disposer audit on the hot path).
- **D3 While you drag, the world waits** (§4.2). *You notice:* during a slider drag the map previews each value but the 3D world neither changes nor loads new terrain; it starts regenerating from the value you release on. Technically: one pool and one generator context; the map's configure driver is the only caller of `configure` after the reveal and adopts the page's first configure; the coordinator issues world jobs only while `pool.epoch` equals the generation it applied, so a drag's intermediate drafts never generate a world column. Alternative: two contexts per worker addressed by a config id (the world keeps loading on the applied settings during a drag, at the cost of a second generator context and a config field on every job), or a second pool (2 × N workers and two configures per edit; contradicts master §10's "one pool").
- **D4 Changes apply by themselves, 0.15 s after the last one** (§4.3). *You notice:* no Apply button; the world starts changing about 0.15 s after you stop changing things (after the release, for a drag). A draft is applied when its configure has resolved, nothing newer waits, no gesture is open and 150 ms have passed since the last change; a `beforeApply` seam that always proceeds in SP4c is left for SP5's fork dialog. Alternative: master §5.1's explicit button or 500 ms auto-apply (slower comparisons; the brief wants live).
- **D5 An undo rebuilds only what the undone change had rebuilt** (§4.4). *You notice:* undoing soon after a change is quicker than the change itself, because columns not yet rebuilt are kept. The column's epoch is the interned genKey of the draft its T ran under (a small integer per distinct world, kept for the session); "rebuilds nothing" is "genKey unchanged". The coordinator keeps, per column, the epoch of its voxels and the epochs of the mesh the renderer shows and of one staged but not yet shown. Alternative: a counter bumped by every Apply (an undo would regenerate every column, even those never changed).
- **D6 Each column keeps its old look until its new one is ready** (§4.5). *You notice:* during a change the old world stays in view and is replaced column by column, nearest first; the old terrain in the outermost ring, inside the fog, disappears rather than being rebuilt. Apply frees each stale column and regenerates it from T while its old meshes stay shown: stale voxels are freed at zero pins, nearest first through SP4b's unload order, at most 16 per frame; no Published final set is rewritten in place, so SP5's sim worker stays the first such writer; staged but unrevealed stale payloads are dropped. Alternative: regenerate beside the old voxels (a second record per column: double the store at RD 16, and the torus has one record per slot) or rewrite Published final sets in place under the pins.
- **D9 No holes during a change** (§5.1-§5.3). *You notice:* each column switches from old to new in one frame; no column goes empty while it is rebuilt. `stage` on a column that shows rows stages a replacement, uploaded hidden beside the old rows; in the frame its last row is uploaded the old rows are hidden and the new shown in one call; `unstage(cx, cz)` drops a pending replacement and keeps what is shown. Alternative: hide the old column at once and reveal the new later (a hole per column during every Apply; against the brief).
- **D10 After a far teleport, new terrain does not wait for the old to be cleared** (§5.4). *You notice:* the first terrain at the destination appears up to about 3.7 s sooner at 60 Hz (about 0.9 s at 240 Hz, the Iris Xe's display). The pump disposes emptied regions outside the budget and gives releases at most half the row cap while uploads wait; SP4b's releases-first order would spend about 220 frames releasing old rows before the first new row. Alternative: keep SP4b's order (teleport is visibly late on a 60 Hz screen) or uploads strictly first (old geometry could pile up under continuous uploads).
- **D11 Who gets the workers: the map or the world** (§6). *You notice:* in mode B while the world regenerates, the map's preview refines more slowly (one tile at a time) and the world streams at full speed; in mode A with the map open the map is as fast as `?map`, and the 3D keeps streaming behind it on at least one worker. Two lanes in the pool: world jobs in a FIFO (≤ N, ordered by the coordinator), everything else in a binary heap by (priority, id); points, landing and probes first and uncapped; while the 3D is visible and the world can issue, master §4.3's cap (other map, slice and stats jobs ≤ 1 while any current-epoch T is pending) and world first; while the map covers the 3D (mode A, map open), map jobs first on at most N − 1 workers (master §5.6's "reduced priority"); while the world is held by an unapplied draft (D3), map jobs uncapped. The driver's post-settle spawn search runs in the world at priority 600 under the cap. Alternative: master §4.3's cap at all times (the map refines one tile at a time while the user is looking only at it), or a fixed split of the workers.
- **D12 You arrive about 24 blocks above the real ground** (§3.2). *You notice:* a teleport never leaves you inside a mountain, jagged peaks included. Teleport lands at min(400, max(surface, water) + 24), from a `landing` worker job; `surface` is max(⌊surfaceEst⌋, `surfaceEst3`) whenever the density tree has a reachable `terrain` tap (the default tree has one), else ⌊surfaceEst⌋; yaw, pitch and speed kept, velocity zeroed, x and z clamped to ±500,000. **This departs from the brief's wording** ("the height comes from the map's surface estimate", decision 6): the map's 2D estimate is the same value on smooth ground, but ignores the jaggedness, overhang and detail the 3D terrain adds above it, up to about 30 blocks inside a default-profile jagged peak (the jag spline reaches 55 blocks, `core/params/shapeDefaults.ts:13`) and up to 57 under an amplified tree (SP3e :684); the 3D estimate keeps what the brief wanted from the map's (known before the terrain exists, one worker query). The spawn's rule is unchanged (the spawn goldens stay). Confirmed by the user (Point 4). Alternative: the 2D `surfaceEst` the hover readout already holds (the brief's words; synchronous, sometimes inside a mountain).
- **D13 No flash of daylight in a cave while its column is rebuilt** (§3.4, §4.5). *You notice:* in a cave during a change, the light does not jump to full brightness while your column regenerates; after a teleport you are in open sky. The eye's sky light keeps its last value while the camera's column is not Published, and is 15 right after a teleport. Alternative: SP4b's 15 over any unpublished column (a cave view flashes bright for the regeneration time).
- **D14 A plain click teleports; the map's other clicks move to Shift+click** (§3.1; Point 3). *You notice:* in the world's map a plain click takes you there, except while the cut tool is armed; the pin, the Density tab's horizontal-slice centre and opening the Cross-section near the cut line need Shift+click. `?map` keeps today's routing. The brief's "a click teleports" makes no exception; the armed cut tool is one because the user arms it with its button for its two points. Confirmed by the user (Point 3). Alternative: no exception, the cut tool's points on Shift+click too; or a teleport modifier or button (against the brief's "a click").
- **D15 One owner of the keys, and undo works from the 3D too** (§2.3, §2.4). *You notice:* in mode B, WASD fly only after you click the 3D half, and Esc gives the keys back to the map; Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y work whether the map is shown or not, with a short "Undo"/"Redo" note in the 3D when the map is hidden. One controls owner, 'world' or 'map': mode A open → map; mode B → map until a click on the 3D half locks the pointer, map again when the lock is lost; the world's keys, wheel and flight act only for 'world', M in both (not in text entry); P and Esc act on the map only while it owns the controls. Alternative: undo and redo only while the map is shown (no comparison from the 3D in mode A with Watch live).
- **D16 The two layouts** (§2.1, §2.2). *You notice:* mode A covers the whole window; mode B splits it in two fixed halves, map left and 3D right, and is offered only on a window at least 1,000 px wide (narrower, the switch shows why, and a remembered B opens as A). Mode A is an opaque overlay over the full-size 3D (no resize), whose frames are capped at 60 fps while covered; mode B moves the 3D host to the right half with an explicit resize and render at the switch, the map's 899 px breakpoint becoming a container query on its host. Alternatives: a draggable, stored split; hiding the 3D with `display: none` (a 1 × 1 drawing buffer and a black frame on return); mode B offered at any width, the map's panels stacked under the map in a half narrower than 500 px (usable on a laptop at 1,366 px, cramped below).
- **D17 The mode and Watch live are remembered with the world's settings** (§2.5). *You notice:* the map reopens in the mode you left it in, also after a reload. `mapMode: 'full' | 'split'` (default `'full'`, the user's description) and `watchLive: boolean` (default true, as in the mockup) in `wi.world.settings`; the map's own layout stays in `wi10.layout.v1`, shared with `?map`. Alternative: in the layout key (the standalone page has no modes, and its writes would drop the fields).
- **D18 Watch live switches to the 3D when the change applies, not while you drag** (§2.1). *You notice:* the map never vanishes under your mouse in the middle of a drag; a change that alters nothing leaves the map up. Alternative: on the input (the drag is cut off at its first pixel).
- **D19 The map opens centred on you** (§1.5). *You notice:* every M opens the map centred on the camera at the last zoom, with a "Centre on me" button; the arrow follows the camera smoothly in mode B (redrawn at most 30 times a second). Alternative: reopen at the last view (the brief: "centred on the camera").
- **D20 The address bar follows your edits and teleports** (§1.6). *You notice:* a reload (F5) or a copied URL reopens the edited world at the place you last teleported to. The world's URL is `pathname + search + '#' + hash`, the hash the applied session, debounced 200 ms through `common/urlWriter.ts`; a teleport also rewrites `?at` to its destination (flight itself does not); the Reload buttons read the live session and the camera; no `hashchange` in the world; the help's "Open the map here" becomes "Map (M)". Alternative: the URL as entered (a context-loss Reload loses every live edit); or `?at` left as entered (F5 after a teleport returns to the entry point).
- **D23 SP4c's size and what may slip** (§13). *You notice:* if time runs short, two things may arrive in SP10 instead: a faster map preview while the world regenerates (the climate-grid cache) and the map header's "Regenerating n / needed" line. SP4c is L; the scope is the map refactor, two modes with a pointer-lock hand-off, teleport, Apply with the swap in two renderers, the per-column epoch, a two-lane pool and new metric and bench legs. Mode B is not on the cut line: it is the user's chosen deliverable ("live editing in both modes"). Alternative: keep M (the master's estimate) and cut mode B (against the brief).
- **D24 What "within a couple of seconds" and "instant" mean in numbers** (§8.4). *You notice:* nothing directly; these are the pass marks of the exit. The user's "compare before and after within a couple of seconds" becomes: after an edit and after its undo, every column within 64 blocks of the camera (the render-distance-4 disc, 69 columns) shows the new world within 2 s (`applyNearMs`, `undoNearMs` ≤ 2,000 ms), on the Iris Xe in Chrome. "Instant" teleport becomes: the first terrain within 2 columns (32 blocks) of the camera appears within 0.5 s of the click (`teleportFirstRevealMs` ≤ 500 ms). The whole visible disc's times are recorded, not checked. Alternatives: the whole visible disc (RD 12, 192 blocks) within N s (for example 5 s for an edit, 3 s for a teleport); or record the times only and let the user's check judge them.
- **D25 The night fog is not pulled into SP4c** (Non-goals). *You notice:* at night the streaming edge stays as visible as at the close of SP4b. It is a colour and density tuning of the night sky, unrelated to any SP4c mechanism, and pulling it would add a visible change to judge beside the two modes. Alternative: pull it in as a small tuning (the night fog closes as the day fog does), about a day's work and one more thing to judge at the user's check.
- **D26 The small additions you will see** (§2.1, §2.5, §3.2, §4.5). These are the spec's own, not the brief's: the HUD line "stale n / needed" while the world is being rebuilt; in mode A with Watch live, a notice over the 3D "Regenerating… near → far · M returns to the map" while it is stale (from the approved mockup); the map header's hint "Click: go here · Shift+click: pin" (the mockup's "clic = ir aquí"); the notice "Could not teleport" when the landing job fails; the 1.5 s "Undo"/"Redo" notice in the 3D (D15). The map header's "Regenerating n / needed" line is on the cut line (D23). Alternative: none of them (the user reads the regeneration only from the terrain), or the mockup's two hints only.
- **D27 A failure closes the map, and the world's notices reach you over it** (§2.1). *You notice:* if the world fails (a crash, a lost graphics context) while the map covers it, the map closes and the failure panel or the context-loss notice shows; the world's other notices (storage full, URL too long, Could not teleport) appear in the map's notice row while the map covers the 3D. Alternative: raise the world's notice area above the overlay (it lives inside the 3D host, so in mode B it would cover the map's right edge).

### Technical decisions (no visible effect)

## 3. Teleport

### 3.1 The click (D14)

`mapClickAction` (`ui/map/clickAction.ts:35-40`) gains the input `host: 'world' | 'page'` and the action `teleport`. In the world (the user's answer to Point 3): an armed cut tool takes the plain click; any other plain click teleports to the clicked block: the landing job is asked at (⌊x⌋, ⌊z⌋), since `surfaceEst3` takes integer positions (`gen/column/surfaceEstimate.ts:30-31`, RangeError otherwise), and the camera goes to (⌊x⌋ + 0.5, ⌊z⌋ + 0.5) (§3.2); Shift+click runs today's routing for an unarmed tool as it is (`clickAction.ts:37-39`: with the Density tab shown, a horizontal plane sets its centre and a vertical plane sets the pin, even near the cut line; with the Density tab not shown, a click near the cut line opens Cross-section, else it sets the pin). On `?map` (`host: 'page'`) nothing changes. A click is still a pointerup without movement since pointerdown (`mapView.ts:253-259`).

### 3.2 The landing height (D12)

- **`landing(x, z)`**, a new worker job (`workers/protocol.ts`, `taskHandler.ts`) at the clicked block (⌊x⌋, ⌊z⌋): x and z are integers (the message guard refuses anything else, as `surfaceEst3` takes integer positions, `gen/column/surfaceEstimate.ts:30-31`) and a position outside the world window is BAD_ARGS. It reads the terrain cell, runs at priority −2 in the aux lane ahead of everything, is exempt from the aux cap (§6.2) and answers `{ surface, water }`. `surface` is `landingSurface(ctx, x, z)`, exported from `gen/column/spawn.ts` beside `spawnY` and built from the same parts (`CREATE_DC`, `FIND_TAP`, `surfaceEst3`): max(⌊surfaceEst⌋, `surfaceEst3`) whenever the density tree has a reachable `terrain` tap, **the default tree included**, else ⌊surfaceEst⌋. Under the default tree the surface rises above the 2D estimate by the jaggedness (the jag spline reaches 55 blocks, `core/params/shapeDefaults.ts:13`), σ·overhang and detail (SP3b's `terrain` tap), so the 2D estimate alone can leave the camera about 30 blocks inside a jagged peak; the max keeps the 2D value where the 8-step scan returns ground under an overhang. `water` is `surfaceWaterLevel` (63 over ocean and river, the lake's level, −∞ elsewhere). `spawnY` is unchanged (⌊surfaceEst⌋ + 1 under the default tree, SP3e's rule otherwise): the spawn goldens stay byte-identical. This departs from the brief's "the map's surface estimate" (D12, Point 4).
- **The camera's y** = min(400, max(surface, water) + 24): over the sea 87; over plains near 64 about 90; over a 300-block peak 324. x and z are clamped to ±500,000 (`ui/world/query.ts`'s `AT_LIMIT`, master §2.1).
- **Cost:** one job. It waits for the first worker to free, whatever that worker runs: usually a world job (2-5 ms), at worst the shortest of the N running jobs (a preview tile ≈ 54 ms, SP2b :232, or a shares part), about 60 ms with map tiles in flight. It computes in a few milliseconds (the 2D estimate plus about 10 density probes), so the camera usually moves within a frame or two of the click and at worst within about 0.1 s; the bench records it (`teleportFirstRevealMs`, §8.4). A rejection (`JobCancelled` when a configure moved the generation) is retried up to 3 times; a worker failure shows the notice "Could not teleport" (D26) and nothing moves.
- **Floating islands:** `surfaceEst3` starts from the ground's offset, so under an island it can return the ground below it; the camera then arrives in the open air between the ground and the island (y 192-264), which is accepted for flight.
- No cache on the quart lattice: one query per click needs none (master §3.7 amended, §12).

### 3.3 The move (`ui/world/teleport.ts`)

`teleport(x, z)` awaits `landing`, then sets `fly.x`, `fly.z`, `fly.y`, zeroes `vx`, `vy`, `vz` and keeps `yaw`, `pitch` and `speed`. The next tick's `coordinator.update` does the rest (SP4b §3.4): one column or a thousand, the slots re-classify, the old meshes outside keepMesh are hidden (64 per frame), the old voxels are freed in the order of the T jobs they unblock (16 per frame, nearest first, SP4b §3.5), the heaps issue nearest-first, and the floating origin rebases by itself. Old columns still within the new keepMesh and keepVoxels discs (a short teleport) are kept. With D10's pump (§5.4) the first new rows upload in the first frames instead of after the old rows' releases. A teleport during an Apply regenerates the new place under the applied epoch; an old column freed by the Apply but not yet regenerated, whose old mesh is still shown, has that mesh removed when its slot moves to another column (§4.5's sink column), so no ghost geometry stays at the old place.

### 3.4 The eye light and the fog (D13; Point 1)

The eye's sky light (`frameStep.ts:85-91`) keeps its last value while `store.lightAt` returns −1 (the camera's column is not Published: freed by an Apply, or not yet generated); after a teleport it is reset to 15 (the camera arrives 24 blocks above the surface, in open sky). The fog does not react to a teleport (the user's answer to Point 1; the alternative, not built, was the fog closing in around the camera and opening out as the terrain fills in).

### 3.5 Walk mode's rule, recorded for SP5

In walk mode (SP5) a teleport places the feet at the `landing` job's surface + 1, keeps the look direction, hovers while the column is unloaded and snaps to `MOTION_BLOCKING` once the column is Published (master §3.7, amended §12).

## 4. Apply v1

### 4.1 Scopes and `engine/invalidation.ts` (D8)

- Every schema leaf is climate- or terrain-scope (`core/params/schema.ts:8-11`); the decorate, light, mesh, lod and map stages hash no parameter (`core/stage/registry.ts:15-26`); `stageHashes` ignore the seed and `genKey` covers the seed and the decorate hash, which chains climate, shape, surfaceEst, biome2d and terrain (`core/stage/hash.ts:14-34`). So "the world differs" is exactly "genKey differs", and every world-changing Apply in SP4c re-runs T and everything after it.
- **`regenFrom(prev: AppliedDraft, next: AppliedDraft): WorldStage | null`**, `WorldStage = 'T' | 'D' | 'L' | 'MESH'`, `AppliedDraft = { seedKey: string; hashes: Readonly<Partial<Record<StageId, string>>>; genKey: string }`, in hex as the workers report them (`ReadyMsg.stageHashes` and `genKey`, `workers/protocol.ts:142`; `StageHashes` holds `Hash64` tuples, `core/stage/hash.ts:8`): a different seed → 'T'; else the first stage of `STAGES` (`core/stage/registry.ts:15-26`) whose hex differs or is missing on either side, as `dirtyStages` (`hash.ts:37-43`) does for `Hash64`, mapped to the earliest world stage (climate, shape, surfaceEst, biome2d or terrain → 'T'; decorate → 'D'; light → 'L'; mesh → 'MESH'); lod and map only, or nothing → null. `appliedDraftOf(seedKey, ready)` builds one from a resolved configure; `epochOf` interns the genKey hex (§4.4). A profile switch is a patch change and needs nothing of its own (master §5.1's footnote). Unit-tested on synthetic hashes for every stage. SP10 completes it for every scope.
- **SP4c's coordinator regenerates from T for any non-null answer** (a superset of what 'D', 'L' and 'MESH' need, correct by construction); the narrower paths are SP8b's and SP10's (§11). In SP4c only 'T' and null are reachable.

### 4.2 The configure owner and the generation gate (D3)

- A task worker holds one generator context, rebuilt by every `configure` (`workers/taskHandler.ts:183`, `:204-218`), and the map previews the draft by configuring the pool for every draft, a drag's included (`ui/map/previewDriver.ts:153`). On one pool, the map's draft **is** what the workers compute. So the world must not issue jobs while the pool holds a draft it has not applied.
- **The configure driver** (`previewDriver.ts`, generalised) is created by the world page at the reveal and is the only caller of `pool.configure` after it. New members: `adopt({ poolEpoch, sessionEpoch, seedKey, ready })` takes over the page's first configure (today the first `change` always configures; the seed key is needed because the canvas's `setSource(poolEpoch, seedKey, hashes)`, `previewDriver.ts` `DriverCanvas`, `mapView.ts:45`, cannot be replayed without it, and `DriverPool.configure` resolves `{ epoch, stageHashes, genKey }`, the `genKey` the driver dropped until now); `setPreviewWanted(on)`, false while no map is shown: a cycle then lands as soon as its configure resolves (today it waits for the preview to draw, or the ≥ 1 s watchdog; an Apply itself needs only a resolved configure and an empty slot, but a second edit made from the 3D while the first waits in the slot, `previewDriver.ts:136-140`, would wait up to a second for a preview nobody sees); `onConfigured(fn)`, called with `{ poolEpoch, sessionEpoch, seedKey, ready: { stageHashes, genKey }, gesture, urgent }` for every resolved configure; `status.queued` (a newer draft waits in the slot); `dispose()` (the watchdog cleared, late configures ignored).
- **A replaying canvas sink.** Today the driver pushes its source into the canvas only at configure time (`canvas.setSource`, `previewDriver.ts:159`) and the spawn after a settle (`:104`); a canvas created later has no source, its `keyOf` returns null and `request()` returns early (`mapView.ts:96`, `:104-108`), so a map mounted at the first M would stay blank until the next edit. The driver's `canvas` becomes a forwarding sink that keeps the last source, interactivity and spawn and replays them into a canvas attached later (`attach(canvas)`, `detach()`); the tool attaches at mount and detaches at dispose. Unit test: after an adopt, a fake canvas attached later receives the adopted source (epoch, seed key, hashes), then the interactivity and the spawn. That the first `show()` after an adopt plans tiles cannot run in Node (`mapView.request` needs a 2D context and a DOM, `vitest.config.ts:11`): the browser smoke checks it, "tiles drawn within 5 s without any edit" (§8.6).
- **The gate:** `CoordinatorJobs` gains `generation(): number` (the pool's `epoch`); the coordinator issues a world job only while `generation() === appliedGeneration`. Any configure the world has not applied therefore holds the world: queued world jobs were rejected by the configure itself (`workerPool.ts:495-508`), running ones stop at the moved cells or finish (§4.6), and the coordinator issues nothing more; old meshes stay shown, the map keeps working on the aux lane.

### 4.3 When a draft is applied (`ui/world/applyControl.ts`, D4)

On the model of SP4b's live render distance (one control; the change kept as state and acted on at the next frame; latest wins):
- `createApplyControl({ coordinator, driver, session, pool, clock, quietMs: 150, beforeApply, onApplied })` keeps the latest `Configured` and, once per frame (`tick()`, allocation-free at rest: a few scalar compares, SP4b D25), applies it when all hold: its `poolEpoch` equals `pool.epoch` (no newer configure was issued), `driver.status.queued` is false, the session is not inside a gesture, its `sessionEpoch` is the session's current epoch, and 150 ms have passed since the session's last change (`clock`).
- **Applying:** `from = regenFrom(applied, next)`; `beforeApply(from)` (SP4c: always proceeds; SP5's fork dialog suspends auto-apply here, master §2.7 (b)); then `from === null` → `coordinator.adopt(poolEpoch)`, else `coordinator.apply({ epoch: epochOf(next.genKey), generation: poolEpoch, from })`; `applied = next`; `onApplied({ from })`, which drives Watch live (§2.1), the URL (§1.6) and the undo notice (§2.4).
- **A burst keeps the last:** a drag is one gesture whose drafts are non-urgent; the world holds through all of them (§4.2) and applies only the draft configured after the release. Urgent changes in quick succession (Ctrl+Z held, arrow keys on a focused slider) are collapsed by the driver's one-cycle-at-a-time slot and the 150 ms quiet window. Any Apply supersedes an earlier one at once: nothing waits for the previous regeneration to finish.
- **A configure failure** after the reveal is fatal (the failure panel with "Copy details"): the session validates every draft (tier 2 included) before the driver sees it, so a refused configure is a bug.
- The bench drives the same control with a scripted session and no map (§8.4).

### 4.4 The per-column epoch (D5)

- `epochOf(genKey)`: a session-wide `Map` from the genKey's hex to a small integer, from 1 (the first entry's draft), one entry per distinct world applied (about 50 bytes each).
- The slot table (SP4b §3.1) gains, each an `Int32Array(4096)`:
  - `colEpoch`: the epoch the column's T was issued under; 0 for Absent;
  - `shownEpoch`: the epoch of the payload the renderer shows for the slot's sink column; 0 when nothing is shown;
  - `stagedEpoch`: the epoch of a payload staged but not yet shown (a first reveal or a swap pending); 0 when none;
  - `sinkCx`, `sinkCz`: the column whose rows the renderer holds for this slot (shown or staged), set at `stage`, cleared at `remove`; `NO_COLUMN` when it holds nothing. They differ from the slot's tracked column (`tcx`, `tcz`) only for a column freed with its old mesh kept (§4.5).
  The coordinator does not hear about reveals, so it keeps the two mesh epochs exact through the sink's `unstage`, which returns whether it dropped a pending payload: `true` → `stagedEpoch = 0` (the shown payload stays); `false` → the staged payload is already shown, `shownEpoch = stagedEpoch, stagedEpoch = 0`. The epoch the column will show once its uploads finish is `stagedEpoch || shownEpoch` (its *target*). `remove` clears both. `stage` sets `stagedEpoch = wEpoch` and is only ever called with `stagedEpoch` 0: a column is staged at most once per epoch (SP4b's "never re-staged" rule, now per epoch), and `apply` resolves every pending payload of another epoch before the next stage (§4.5); a debug assertion checks it. The coordinator keeps `wEpoch`, the applied epoch.
- **Eligibility** (SP4b §3.3) adds the epoch: D needs its 3 × 3 at ≥ Proto **with `colEpoch` = `wEpoch`**, L its 3 × 3 at ≥ Decorated with the same, MESH its 3 × 3 Published with the same (master §5.1's "meshing waits until all 3 × 3 columns share the epoch", applied to D and L too); MESH also needs the column's target ≠ `wEpoch`. T is issued under `wEpoch`. A Published column at `wEpoch` whose shown mesh is already at `wEpoch` (and nothing else staged) is marked `meshed` without a MESH: this is how an undo before the new mesh appeared keeps the old one, and how MESH's `done` stays defined.
- **Stale** means (stage ≥ 1 and `colEpoch ≠ wEpoch`) or (the target is non-zero and ≠ `wEpoch`). A column not yet generated (Absent with nothing shown) is not stale: it is simply needed.
- **Kept columns:** a column with `colEpoch = wEpoch` is current; its mesh is current when also its target = `wEpoch`. After an undo from B back to A, every column never regenerated as B is current again and nothing of it re-runs; the columns already regenerated as B are stale and regenerate as A (their light and mesh depended only on neighbours that are, or will again be, A).

### 4.5 `coordinator.apply` (D6)

`apply({ epoch, generation, from })` sets `wEpoch = epoch` and `appliedGeneration = generation`, then in one pass over the 4,096 slots (≤ 1 ms, as a crossing; no allocation):
- **Pending payloads of another epoch** (`stagedEpoch` non-zero and ≠ `wEpoch`): `sink.unstage(sinkCx, sinkCz)`, resolved as §4.4 (dropped, or found already shown). A payload staged at `wEpoch` (an undo back to the epoch pending) stays.
- **Stale columns** (stage ≥ 1 and `colEpoch ≠ wEpoch`): `meshSeq` bumped (a MESH in flight is dropped at intake); they become freeable **inside** keepVoxels (SP4b's `freeable`, `coordinator.ts:715-718`, required outside), still at zero pins and not busy. Their frees go through SP4b's unload heap, keyed by the column's own T score, at most 16 per frame (`FREES_PER_FRAME`), so the nearest regenerate first; a free calls `store.freeColumn` and returns the slot to Absent but **keeps the shown mesh** (no `sink.remove`; `sinkCx`, `sinkCz` and `shownEpoch` stay). `unloadCheck` (`coordinator.ts:686-691`) also queues a stale column at stage ≥ 1 into the free heap inside keepVoxels, so a column that was busy at `apply` (and left the heap) is queued again by the settle that ends its job, instead of staying stale for ever.
- **The sink column:** every mesh removal targets the sink column, not the tracked one: `unmesh` calls `sink.remove(sinkCx[s], sinkCz[s])`, and the mesh-unload heap (H_MESH_OUT) keys on "the sink holds a column outside keepMesh" (`sinkCx ≠ NO_COLUMN`), now separate from `meshed`. Before `classify` lets an Absent slot `follow` a new window column (`coordinator.ts:649-653`, which overwrites `tcx`/`tcz`, `:671-675`), it removes the mesh of a sink column the slot will no longer track (`sink.remove` only queues the rows' release, which the pump paces, §5.4; at most the MESH set's columns, as many as a teleport already hides). So a freed, not yet regenerated column whose old mesh is still shown never leaves its mesh behind when a teleport or fast flight (about 19 columns at 384 blocks/s) moves the camera on: no ghost geometry, no leaked GPU memory. In SP4b an Absent slot never had a shown mesh (the free path always unmeshed first, `:765-774`), so the question did not arise.
- **Stale meshes outside the MESH set** (the keepMesh ring, RD + 1) are hidden (`sink.remove`, ≤ 64 per frame): no MESH will replace them, and they lie in the fog.
- **Current columns** whose target ≠ `wEpoch` lose `meshed` and get a new MESH when eligible; Published current columns whose target = `wEpoch` are marked `meshed` (§4.4).
- The heaps are rebuilt from the camera. `adopt(generation)` only sets `appliedGeneration` and requeues: no epoch changes, nothing is freed or staged.
- **Counts** (`counts(out)`): each stage's `done` counts only current columns; new fields `stale` (MESH-set columns that are stale by §4.4's definition: a column not yet generated is not counted) and `epoch` (`wEpoch`). The HUD shows "stale n / needed" while it is above 0.
- **No Published final set is rewritten in place:** every stale column is freed at zero pins and claimed again by its new T, so SP4b §12.1's "Apply rewrites Published columns" does not arise; SP5's sim worker stays the first writer of Published final sets (master §4.1 and risk 1, §12). Voxel memory stays flat (free before T); GPU memory holds old and new rows only of the columns mid-swap (§5.1).

### 4.6 Intake and the pool's world replies (D7)

- **Today** a successful reply is settled as `JobCancelled` when the pool's generation moved after the job's issue (`workerPool.ts:437-445`); for a D or L that committed before the configure arrived, the coordinator requeues it at its old stage, the next `reopenColumn` throws (the store is a stage ahead, `store.ts:108-113`), and after two failures the column is skipped: a hole on the first Apply with work in flight.
- **SP4c:** a world job (`terrain`, `decorate`, `light`, `meshColumn`) whose worker replied OK resolves with its outcome and its `generation` (the job's `epoch`), whatever the pool's generation is now; ABORTED and STALE_EPOCH replies still reject with `JobCancelled` (nothing was committed). Map, point, landing, stats, slice and probe jobs keep today's rule.
- **The coordinator** captures `wEpoch` per in-flight job at issue (a `busyEpoch` column beside `busy`) and judges at intake: T, D and L results are recorded as the store now stands (the table follows the store's truth); a column whose job ran under another epoch is stale by its `colEpoch` and is freed by §4.5's path once unpinned. A MESH result whose `busyEpoch ≠ wEpoch` is dropped (and its `meshSeq` no longer matches anyway). Results of jobs issued under a generation the world later adopted with the same genKey are accepted: the content is the same world.

### 4.7 Seed and profile changes

Engine-wise a seed or profile change is a terrain Apply: a new genKey, every column stale, nearest-first regeneration, the camera unmoved. The driver's post-settle spawn search (`previewDriver.ts:103-121`) feeds the map's spawn marker and runs in the world at priority 600 in the aux lane (§6.3).

### 4.8 Undo and redo

The session's history (`engine/session.ts:59-66`, 101 snapshots) lives in the world page's session, so it survives the map's hide and show. Undo and redo end a gesture and emit urgent changes (`:357-366`), which reach the world through the driver and `applyControl` like any edit (§4.3): within 150 ms of the key plus one configure the world starts regenerating, nearest first, and the columns never changed by the undone step are kept (§4.4). The time to see the near area again is recorded and checked at the exit (§8.4).

### 4.9 `bumpScope`, `configure` and ENG-4 (D8)

- **Apply is one `configure`**, which stores the new generation into all four epoch cells; with every SP4c leaf terrain- or climate-scope, "bump from the dirty stage onward" (master §2.3) is all four cells, so `bumpScope` adds nothing and is not called. It stays built and unit-tested for SP8b's decorate-scope Apply (§11).
- **ENG-4 closed now:** after `bumpScope('light')` at generation g the cells read [g, g, g + 1, g + 1], and today's next `configure` stores g + 1 into all four, so an L or MESH issued in between (scope g + 1) is never stopped. `configure` now sets the generation to `max(epoch + 1, max(cells) + 1)` before storing it, from the pool's second configure on, so the new value differs from every value any job was issued with; the first configure stays 0 (the pool's `epoch` starts at −1 in `createWorkerPool` and the cells at 0, `world/store/epochs.ts:22-24`, a value issued to no job, so the formula would give 1 and move SP4b's pool tests' epochs); without cells it is `epoch + 1` as before; a pool test covers it. Tile keys (`mapView.ts:96`) only need the generation to change, which it does.

## 5. The renderers' swap and the pump

### 5.1 The contract (`render/sectionRenderer.ts`, `regionGrid.ts`; D9)

- **`stage(p, seq)` on a column that shows rows** (revealed) stages a replacement: the staging's per-column record keeps the shown payload as `prev`, which stays visible and stays counted in its region (so a growth, an optimisation or a compaction moves it, and the region is not disposed); the new payload's rows are uploaded hidden beside it, nearest-first through the ready queue (SP4b §3.9). **In the frame its last row is uploaded**, `prev`'s rows are hidden and the new rows shown in one call; `prev`'s rows are then released under the budget like a removal's. A replacement whose payload has no row hides `prev` at once (an all-air column).
- **On an unrevealed column** `stage` replaces the staged payload as today; a second `stage` during a swap replaces the pending replacement and keeps `prev`.
- **`unstage(cx, cz)`** drops a staged, unrevealed payload (its uploaded rows hidden and released under the budget) and keeps whatever is shown; a no-op when nothing is staged.
- **`remove(cx, cz)`** during a swap hides both `prev` and the replacement.
- During `holdReveals` (the first entry) nothing is revealed, so nothing swaps.
- **The probe** (SP4b §8.1) counts `partial` over shown columns (some but not all of a payload's non-empty rows visible) and `mixed` (a column showing rows of two seqs); the swap keeps both at 0 by construction.

### 5.2 RegionBatch

The replacement's rows are `addGeometry` plus `addInstance` hidden (`setVisibleAt(false)`), with the reservation, growth and optimisation rules of SP4b §4.2 (each a structural operation); at the swap, one pass sets `prev`'s instances invisible and the new ones visible. `prev`'s rows are then deleted (`deleteGeometry`) under the row cap. An Apply over a whole region deletes and re-adds most of its geometry, so SP4b's 40 % rule turns into `optimize()` (3.2-3.5 ms CPU measured on the Iris Xe, SP4b §4.2) roughly once per region and pass: about 20-40 structural operations for an RD 12 Apply, one per frame at most. The bench measures their frames (§8.4).

### 5.3 RegionMesh

The replacement takes new vertex and index ranges from the arena's free list (first fit), its index range zero until the swap; at the swap, in the same frame, the new range's rebased indices are written and `prev`'s index range zeroed (two update ranges, `addUpdateRange` plus `needsUpdate`, SP4b §4.3); `prev`'s ranges are freed under the budget. Old and new coexist only per column mid-swap, so the arena grows (×1.5) or compacts (≥ 40 % free) as in SP4b; the region's culling sphere is the union of the shown spans, `prev` included until it is hidden.

### 5.4 The pump's order (D10)

SP4b's pump (`regionGrid.ts:337-436`) releases first, counting every released row against the 32-row cap. After a far teleport at RD 12 about 489 old columns of 10-20 rows are removed, about 7,000 rows, which at 32 per frame keep every new row out for about 220 frames on top of generation: 3.7 s at 60 Hz, 1.8 s at 120 Hz, 0.9 s at 240 Hz (the Iris Xe's display, `displayIntervalMs` 4.165 in `assets/sp4b/bench-iris-chrome.json`); during an Apply, each swap releases about as many rows as it uploads. SP4c's order per frame:
1. **Emptied regions are disposed outright:** a region whose every column is removed (none shown, none staged) is disposed in one step, after its pending removals are purged: the `removing` entries that still hold rows of it (`c.region.holding`, `regionGrid.ts:357-374`) are dropped from the release queue without a per-row release, so no later release touches a disposed region (its `BatchedMesh` or arena geometry disposed), with no per-row release, outside the row cap and outside the structural slot (it uploads nothing; a few `deleteBuffer` calls), at most 8 regions per frame.
2. One structural operation for the nearest pending column (as SP4b).
3. **Releases** of removed rows and swapped-out `prev` rows: at most 16 rows (half the cap) while any staged column waits for an upload, else up to the cap.
4. **Uploads**, nearest-first, with the rest of the 2 ms and 32 rows.
5. Reveals and swaps of complete columns.
After a far teleport the old regions go in the first frames (step 1), and the new rows start uploading as soon as the first MESH results arrive; GPU memory then holds the old place only until its regions are disposed. The loading screen's budget (12 ms, no caps) is unchanged.

### 5.5 The contract cases (both renderers, `test/unit/sectionRendererContract.ts`)

SP4b's case "staging a revealed column throws" (`:295`) is replaced by: a staged replacement keeps every old row visible until the replacement's last row is uploaded, then the old are hidden and the new shown in one pump; a second `stage` during a swap replaces the pending payload and keeps the old one shown; `unstage` during a swap keeps the old one shown and releases the pending rows; `remove` during a swap hides both; an empty replacement hides the old at once; an emptied region is disposed in the next pump without spending a row or the structural slot, also while removals of it are still queued (they are purged, and no release afterwards touches the disposed region); with releases pending and an upload waiting, a pump spends at most 16 rows on releases; on a swap that needs no growth, `gpuBytes()` returns to the old level after the swap's releases (a swap that grew the region compares with the post-growth size, since growth ×1.5 is never undone, SP4b §4.3); the probe's `partial` and `mixed` are 0 on every frame of a randomised stage / re-stage / unstage / remove / pump sequence over revealed columns.

## 6. One worker pool for tiles and world jobs

### 6.1 Two lanes (D11)

The pool's single queue, sorted on every assignment (`workerPool.ts:353-359`, SP4b §2.8's hand-over), becomes two lanes: the **world lane**, a FIFO of world jobs in the coordinator's issue order (at most N queued: the coordinator caps the total), and the **aux lane**, a binary heap (`engine/jobHeap.ts`, `Int32Array` indices over a preallocated key array, grown ×2 when full) ordered by (priority, id): map tiles (preview at `dist`, fine tiles at `1000 + dist`, `viewMath.ts:37-38`), point and probes (−1), landing and spawn (−2, or 600 for the world's spawn), stats and slices (500). `cancelTiles(pred)` and `cancelWorld(pred)` filter their lane (the heap is rebuilt in O(n)). A unit test checks the aux lane yields the same order as today's sort on randomised queues.

### 6.2 The aux policy and the MAP cap (D11)

`pool.setAuxPolicy(first, cap)`, two scalars set by the page every frame from the overlay's state, the gate and the coordinator's counts (no allocation; a no-op when unchanged, §4.10):
- **Interactive jobs** (priority < 0: point, probes, landing, the standalone page's spawn) go first and do not count against the cap.
- **The world held** (`pool.epoch ≠ appliedGeneration`: a drag, or a draft not yet applied, §4.2): `first: 'aux'`, `cap` = N. The world issues nothing then, so capping the map would leave N − 1 workers idle and slow the preview, and with it the driver's cycle and the next edit (`previewDriver.ts:182-187`). This rule comes first.
- **The 3D visible and the world issuing** (map hidden, or mode B): `first: 'world'`, `cap` = 1 while any current-epoch T is pending in the T set (`counts.T.done < counts.T.needed`), else N: master §4.3's "MAP, SLICE, METRIC … ≤ 1 while any T is pending within RD".
- **The map covering the 3D and the world issuing** (mode A, map shown): `first: 'aux'`, `cap` = N − 1 (at least 1): a freed worker takes a waiting map job first, but one worker is always left to the world, which keeps streaming (master §5.6's "the 3D view keeps streaming at reduced priority while the map is open"). The pool pumps synchronously on each reply (`workerPool.ts:437-447`), so with the aux lane first and uncapped every freed worker would take a tile while dozens are queued after a pan or a zoom, and an Apply "applied behind" with Watch live off would make no progress while the user browses the map.
- On the standalone `?map` there is no world lane: `first: 'aux'`, `cap` = N, as today.

### 6.3 Priorities in the world

The world's spawn search after each settle (it feeds the map's spawn marker) runs at priority 600, behind preview tiles and stats and under the cap, instead of −2 ahead of the world's jobs (`workerPool.ts:546-556`); the standalone page keeps −2.

### 6.4 The climate-grid cache, the lever (cut line)

With the 3D visible and T pending, the map's preview runs one tile at a time: four level-256 tiles at about 54 ms each (SP2b :232), about 220 ms plus the configure. SP2b's climate-grid cache (a per-worker cache of the climate grid; a preview tile ≈ 6 ms) is the lever. It is built in SP4c only if the in-world preview, measured in mode B during an Apply on the Iris Xe in the headful production check (the driver's `lastLatencyMs`, §14; the headless smoke's SwiftShader figure is recorded but does not decide), lands later than 500 ms, or the user finds the map slow in mode B at the check; otherwise it goes to SP10 with the cut line (§13).

### 8.5 Unit and integration tests (summary)
