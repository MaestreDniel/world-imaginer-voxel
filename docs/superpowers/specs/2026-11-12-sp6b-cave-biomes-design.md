## Goal

Underground, caves now differ by where they lie. Some caves have moss blocks lining their floors and ceilings, with patches of clay on the floor: **lush caves**, under humid ground at moderate depth. Others are lined with warm brown dripstone blocks on floor and ceiling: **dripstone caves**, under ground far inland at moderate depth. Deep under mountains lies **the abyss**: all of its rock is very dark stone, layered with dark shale, cracked stone and thin faintly violet veins, and its floors are grey ash. Nothing lights it but the faint light floor of SP6a, so it is really dark (night vision shows it). Elsewhere caves stay stone and deepslate. Where one cave biome meets another or plain cave, the change is wavy and speckled, never a straight line or a grid. Wide caves no longer end on the flat black bedrock plane: the deepest caves close on a rolling rock floor of mounds and gullies well above it (Point 2). C switches the X-ray between colouring caves by type (the default) and by cave biome; the strip's legend no longer covers the F3 counts. The map gains a cave-biome layer at a chosen depth, the Density tab a "Cave biome" slice, and the Caves tab a "Colour by" choice and a biome finder. The `cave_heavy` preset appears in New world and in the map, with more and larger caves, to find everything fast. Underneath: a cave-biome picker runs per block from the column's climate, its surface estimate and two border noises; T writes each cave voxel's biome into the `cave` byte's high nibble and a per-quart summary into aux B; a new surface rule (rule 4) skins cave floors and ceilings from it; cave culling hides sections that no straight line of sight from the camera can reach. Measured by B3 (biome shares per 8 × 8 km, borders), R3 (0 wrongly culled; ≥ 50 % culled underground), C1's `cave_heavy` part recorded, C1-C9, L2, S1, DT1, DT2, P1 and two Chrome legs. CI first becomes faster (R23). The generated world changes: `GENERATOR_VERSION` 8 → 9 at the one push that changes generated output, and the goldens are re-recorded (D28).

## Decisions taken by this spec (for the user's review)

### Decisions with a visible effect

- **D1 Three cave biomes, told apart by their blocks** (§3, §4). *You notice:* **lush** caves have moss_block floors two blocks deep, with clay patches on about a fifth of the floor, and moss on about seven tenths of their ceilings; **dripstone** caves have dripstone_block floors and ceilings two blocks deep, with bare stone patches on about a sixth; **the abyss** is dark rock all through (D4), with abyss_ash floors on about four fifths of the floor. Walls of lush and dripstone caves stay stone or deepslate (D5). Plain caves are unchanged. Seven new blocks (D36); one cracked block, cracked_abyss_stone, stands for the brief's "cracked variants" (a second, for example a cracked shale, is a one-line addition at tuning if the review asks for it). Alternative: one block per biome (moss, dripstone, abyss_stone), which makes each biome recognisable but drops the brief's "cracked variants, shale strata, ash floors".
- **D2 Where each cave biome lies** (§1). *You notice:* lush caves under humid ground and dripstone caves under ground far inland, both between about 26 and 115 blocks under the surface; the abyss deep under mountains (at least about 128 blocks under the surface, never under the sea). Measured with the master's boxes on today's caves (no bottom floor yet): lush 17.6 %, dripstone 13.4 %, abyss 5.6 % and plain 63.4 % of cave air (recon-gen §2). The master's registry (:770-775) is kept as the defaults. Where lush and dripstone overlap, the one deeper inside its box wins, so the border runs midway. Depth is measured from the column's mean surface height (`surfaceEst`, `src/gen/column/columnStage.ts:105`), so it does not jump at a cliff. Alternative: depth from the local pre-cave top (`surfaceEst3`), which follows cliffs and overhangs but makes biome borders jump where the terrain does.
- **D3 Borders: wavy and speckled, no grid** (§1.3-§1.4). *You notice:* a cave biome ends along a wavy line on the floor, never straight; in depth it ends along a wavy surface; at the line, over about 4-5 blocks, blocks of the two biomes mix in a speckle, so neither carpet ends on a clean edge. Two 2D border noises (λ 64 and 96) shift the climate and the depth that the picker reads, and a per-block jitter of ±2 blocks blurs the line. There is no quart grid at all: the biome is computed per block. Alternative: the master's per-3D-quart picker with a 3D jittered-Voronoi zoom (as the surface biomes' 2D zoom), which gives 3D lobes but costs about twice as much per column and needs the zoom to keep borders off the quart planes (D16).
- **D4 The abyss: dark rock all through, really dark** (§3.3-§3.4). *You notice:* in the abyss, not only the cave skins but all the rock below the abyss's upper surface is abyss rock: abyss_stone layered with abyss_shale, cracked_abyss_stone and thin abyss_stone_veined lines, the layers tilted gently by a noise, so walls, floors and a dug shaft all read as the abyss; the upper surface is the same wavy, speckled border the cave air uses, so the abyss rock and the X-ray's abyss colour agree. Its floors are ash, grey and a little lighter than deepslate ([92, 88, 86] against [78, 78, 84], §4), so the ash floor is the abyss's lightest surface; this is this spec's choice (the brief gives ash no colour), and a darker ash (darker than deepslate, still ≥ 24 from every colour by §4's pairwise rule) is the alternative if the review finds it breaks "very dark". No new block emits light, so the abyss is lit only by SP6a's light floor 0.02 (really dark; night vision, L, shows it) until SP7's lava and SP8b's crystals. Its blocks use the `SOUND` group `abyss` (`src/world/blocks/kinds.ts:79`), unused until now; the silence itself is SP8c's. Alternative: skins only (floors and ceilings), as lush and dripstone; steep abyss walls then stay deepslate, and a vertical shaft into the abyss shows no abyss rock.
- **D5 Lush and dripstone: skins only** (§3.4). *You notice:* floors and ceilings carry the biome's blocks; vertical walls stay stone or deepslate (Minecraft's look for both). Alternative: a third skin on walls, which needs a horizontal-distance condition the rule engine lacks (the scan is per column, `src/gen/surface/scan.ts:181-232`).
- **D6 The bottom of caves: a rock floor** (§5; Point 2). *You notice:* no cave shows the flat bedrock plane; the deepest caves close on a rolling deepslate floor of mounds and gullies, in the abyss on ash over dark rock, on average about 17 blocks above the bedrock (in a wide cavern the air starts at about y −51 in the gullies and up to about y −36 on the mounds; the floor's height changes by about 6-8 blocks over 20-50 blocks; no cave air below y −53 anywhere); smaller caves close rounded at varied heights above it. Digging down still reaches the bedrock at y −64 with SP3c's dither above it. Alternative: Point 2's noisy bedrock layer (F2), or both (F3).
- **D7 The X-ray by cave biome** (§7). *You notice:* C (without Ctrl; Ctrl+C stays Copy) switches the X-ray between "by cave type" (the default) and "by cave biome". In biome mode lush caves show bright green, dripstone amber, the abyss violet and plain caves a dim grey-blue, so the three biomes stand out; the surface film stays grey. The strip and the F3 line read "X-ray · y −64 … 319 · by cave biome" and the legend switches to Plain cave, Lush, Dripstone, Abyss and Surface. The switch is instant, works with the X-ray off (it shows at the next X) and lasts for the page (not saved, like the band). - **D8 The strip no longer covers the F3 counts** (§9.5). *You notice:* with F3 on, the X-ray strip sits to the right of the counts when there is room and below them when not; with F3 off it stays centred at the top. ## 1. The cave-biome picker (D2, D3, D16)

### 1.1 Ids and colours

`CAVE_BIOME = { plain: 0, lush: 1, dripstone: 2, abyss: 3 }` in `src/core/constants.ts`, beside `CAVE_KIND` (gen exports no numeric data, `src/core/constants.ts:169-176`); 4-15 reserved. `CAVE_BIOME_COLORS` (sRGB) holds the X-ray's and the map's colours: plain `#6f86a6`, lush `#4dff7a`, dripstone `#ffb347`, abyss `#b05cff` (chosen to stand out under additive blending on #0b0e14, `src/render/materials/glsl/xray.glsl.ts:63-66`; a dark abyss colour would add almost nothing there; recon-ui B4). The X-ray, the slice field, the Voxels mode and the map layer read this one table, so all four agree.

### 1.2 Inputs per position

For a column with ColumnSample `s` and a position p = (x, z) within J blocks of it (J = `caves.biomes.edgeJitter`, 2; the sample's 7 × 7 lattice with its one-quart halo, `src/gen/column/columnStage.ts:1-4`, `:61-62`, covers 4 blocks on each side, so J ≤ 4 is the halo's limit and the leaf's range):
- `C(p)`, `E(p)`, `H(p)`: `readFieldHalo(s, field, x, z)`, a new quart-bilinear read over the whole lattice, cell indices i, j ∈ −1 … 4, of the CDF-uniform climate fields on [−0.9973, 0.9973] (master :580). It is needed because `readField` (`src/gen/column/columnStage.ts:171-185`) clamps the cell index to the column's cells 0-3 (`:176-177`), so outside the column it extrapolates from the edge cell instead of reading the halo, and two neighbouring columns would compute different values for one position (a seam at every chunk border). `readFieldHalo` uses `readField`'s corner order and lerp order, so it equals `readField` bit for bit inside the column, and two columns that share a position use the same corners and the same fractions, so they agree bit for bit (a unit test over every shared halo position of two neighbours, for C, E, H, `surfaceEst` and Y0). The picker, `caveBiomeAt`, the probes and §3.5's classification all use it.
- `Nb_C(p)`, `Nb_E(p)`, `Nb_H(p)`: the noise `caves.biomes.noises.border` (2D, λ 64, 2 octaves) sampled with three seeds `deriveSeed(seed, 'caveBiome.C' | 'caveBiome.E' | 'caveBiome.H')`, each as z2 / clampSigma clamped to [−1, 1].
- `Nd(p)`: `caves.biomes.noises.depth` (2D, λ 96, 2 octaves), z2 / clampSigma clamped to [−1, 1].
- The perturbed fields: `C′ = C + w·Nb_C`, `E′ = E + w·Nb_E`, `H′ = H + w·Nb_H`, with w = `caves.biomes.borderWarp` (0.05); the climate gradient is about 1/1000 per block, so 0.05 moves a border by up to about ±50 blocks in wiggles of about 64 blocks (**estimate**; B3's `straightBorder` measures the result).
- `Y0(p) = surfaceEst(p) + dW·Nd(p)`, with `surfaceEst(p)` = `readFieldHalo(s, 'surfaceEst', x, z)` (the 2D mean height, the lattice's `l.offset`, `src/gen/column/columnStage.ts:105`) and dW = `caves.biomes.depthWarp` (10 blocks).
- Depth at height y: `depth(p, y) = (Y0(p) − y) / D`, D = `caves.biomes.depthScale` (128, master :586).

The column pass computes these for the (16 + 2J)² = 400 positions once, into preallocated arrays, in a fixed operation order, so two columns compute the same doubles for a shared position (DT1; the rule of `src/gen/column/columnStage.ts:1-4`).

### 1.3 Boxes and the choice

| biome | inside when | margin (uniform units) |
|---|---|---|
| lush | H′ ≥ `lush.hMin` (0.4) and `lush.depthMin` (0.2) ≤ depth ≤ `lush.depthMax` (0.9) | H′ − hMin |
| dripstone | C′ ≥ `dripstone.cMin` (0.6) and `dripstone.depthMin` (0.2) ≤ depth ≤ `dripstone.depthMax` (0.9) | C′ − cMin |
| abyss | E′ ≤ `abyss.eMax` (−0.5) and C′ > `abyss.cMin` (−0.1) and depth ≥ `abyss.depthMin` (1.0) | min(eMax − E′, C′ − cMin) |

The values are the master's registry (:770-775). Among the boxes that contain the point, the one with the largest margin wins; a tie goes to the lower id; in none, plain. With the defaults lush and dripstone overlap where H′ ≥ 0.4 and C′ ≥ 0.6, and their border then runs where H′ − 0.4 = C′ − 0.6, a curve between two independent noise fields; at the defaults the abyss's depth range is disjoint from theirs (a gap of 0.1 depth, about 13 blocks), but the panel's ranges (§6.1) let an edit make any boxes overlap, and the margin rule then decides there too. In the offset splines low E is high ground (`src/core/params/shapeDefaults.ts:15-21`: at E −1 the offset is 72-130 at C 0.05, 80-175 at C 0.3 and 90-210 at C 0.6, rising with PV), so the abyss lies under mountains and uplands. It never holds cave air under the sea: a sea column has surfaceEst < 63, so depth ≥ 1.0 needs y < 63 + 10 − 128 = −55, below the rock floor's lowest air (−53, §5.2).

### 1.4 The per-block jitter

For a voxel v = (x, y, z): three integers jx, jy, jz ∈ [−J, J] from h = `hash3(deriveSeed(seed, 'caveBiome.jitter'), x, y, z) >>> 0` (`src/core/hash.ts:32`): jx = ((h & 1023)·(2J + 1)) >>> 10 − J, jy = (((h >>> 10) & 1023)·(2J + 1)) >>> 10 − J, jz = (((h >>> 20) & 1023)·(2J + 1)) >>> 10 − J (three disjoint 10-bit fields; each value's probability is within 1/1024 of 1/(2J + 1)); `caveBiomeAt(v)` is §1.3's choice at position p′ = (x + jx, z + jz) and height y + jy, read from the column pass's arrays. Borders become a speckle about 2J + 1 = 5 blocks wide, in 3D. J = 0 turns the speckle off. The function is pure in (seed, params, x, y, z).

### 1.5 Where it is evaluated

- At every tagged voxel (the `cave` byte's kind ≠ 0) in T's cave-biome step (§2.1): about 2.3k voxels per column on average (**measured** 9.1 per position, recon-gen §3.3).
- At the floors and ceilings of runs: from the tagged voxel above or below (D18), so no extra evaluation.
- For `caveBiome{here}` (the abyss's rock): through §3.5's per-position classification, evaluated per voxel only within J of the abyss border.
- In the map layer (no jitter: the pixel's position at the chosen y), the slice field, the probe and the finder.

## 3. Surface rules (D18-D21)

### 3.1 The scan

### 3.2 The `caveBiome` condition

- Form: `{ kind: 'caveBiome', side: 'floor' | 'ceiling' | 'here', biomes: CaveBiomeName[] }`, names from `CAVE_BIOME` ('plain', 'lush', 'dripstone', 'abyss'), at least one, no repeats.
- `floor`: true when the voxel's run has a `floorBiome` in `biomes`. `ceiling`: the same with `ceilBiome`. `here`: true when `caveBiomeAt(v)` is in `biomes`.
- Work list: `CONDITION_KINDS` and `FIELDS` (`src/gen/surface/rules.ts:60`, `:180`), validation, both evaluators (`src/gen/surface/compile.ts:238-290`, `src/gen/surface/reference.ts`), the inspector's form, the rule fuzzer's mutators (`treeMutator`).

### 3.3 The `strata` rule kind (D19)

- Form: `{ kind: 'strata', layers: [{ state, weight, maxLen }], minLen, maxLen, noise, amp }`; `noise` a 2D, remap-'none' `surface.noises.*` leaf as `noiseThreshold` accepts (`src/gen/surface/rules.ts:367-377`); 1 ≤ minLen ≤ maxLen ≤ 8; 1-16 layers; weights > 0.
- Table: 192 entries built once per GenContext from `Xoshiro128(deriveSeed(seed, 'surface.strata.' + k))`, k the node's index among the tree's `strata` nodes in depth-first order. From i = 0 while i < 192: draw a layer by weight (one `nextFloat`), then `len = minLen + r.nextInt(min(maxLen, layer.maxLen) − minLen + 1)` (with `layer.maxLen` < minLen, len = layer.maxLen), write it to [i, min(192, i + len)), i += len. No other draw.
- Lookup: `table[((y + o) mod 192 + 192) mod 192]`, `o = Math.round(amp · z / clampSigma)`, z the z2 of `noise` at (x, z), sampled once per position (as `bandlands`, `src/gen/surface/compile.ts:314-329`).
- The abyss's default: layers abyss_stone (weight 50, maxLen 8), abyss_shale (25, 4), cracked_abyss_stone (15, 3), abyss_stone_veined (10, 1, so veins are single lines); minLen 1, maxLen 6; noise `surface.noises.strataOffset` (2D, λ 128, 2 octaves); amp 6.

### 3.5 The fast path's extensions (D20)

`surfaceFastPath` (`src/gen/surface/compile.ts:152-195`) takes a voxel without evaluation when every leaf is depth-bounded or Y-only and the voxel lies beyond the bounds; non-sky runs take their y band's constant state when the tree is sky-gated (`compile.ts:166`, `:392`). Rule 4 breaks both. Four extensions, none changing output:
1. **Ceiling-bounded leaves:** a leaf whose path holds `stoneDepth{ceiling, c}` is bounded by c from the run's bottom; a voxel is fast when its floorDepth exceeds the floor bound and its ceilDepth exceeds the ceiling bound.
2. **Per-class bounds:** one floor bound for leaves under `skyOpen` (today's 4 + SD_MAX = 15) and a separate one for the rest (rule 4's 1). Without it every non-sky run evaluates its top 16 voxels: about 218 floors × 16 ≈ 3.5k voxels, +0.2-0.3 ms (**estimate**, recon-gen §3.5).
3. **Biome-gated skip:** when every non-sky floor (ceiling) leaf has `caveBiome{floor}` (`{ceiling}`) on its path, a run whose `floorBiome` (`ceilBiome`) is none or not named by any such leaf takes the band state. Only biome runs then evaluate: about 76 floors plus as many ceilings × 2-3 voxels ≈ 0.4k voxels (**measured** run counts, recon-gen §1.7).
4. **`caveBiome{here}` classification:** it classifies the picker's result, not one box. Per position, from the column pass's arrays over the (2J + 1)² neighbourhood, each box's climate test is surely true, surely false or mixed, and each box's depth tests fix y ranges where they are surely true or surely false (J above and below the extremes of Y0 − D·depthMin and Y0 − D·depthMax over the neighbourhood). A y range is **surely b** for a biome b when b's box is surely true there and every other box is surely false there (so the margin rule cannot pick another), and **surely not b** when b's box is surely false there. For a `caveBiome{here, biomes}` leaf, voxels in a range surely in `biomes` take the leaf's branch without evaluation (for the abyss's default: the `strata` lookup, and the floor branch at run tops), voxels in a range surely outside `biomes` skip it, and every other voxel evaluates `caveBiomeAt`. With the default boxes the abyss's range is surely abyss wherever its climate test is surely true, because the lush and dripstone depth ranges end 0.1 depth above it; an edit that makes boxes overlap falls back to evaluation in the overlap, which costs time and never changes output. Without this extension the abyss's rock goes through the tree: about 13k voxels per abyss column × 50-80 ns ≈ 0.6-1.0 ms on about 13 % of columns (**estimate**, recon-gen §3.4), in p90 and p99.

A fuzz test (SP3c's pattern) compares the fast path with full evaluation on random trees from `treeMutator`, which learns `caveBiome` (every side and any list of biomes) and `strata`, and on random `caves.biomes.*` values (boxes that overlap, `edgeJitter` 0-4), over columns that hold every biome.

**floating_islands** (`terrain.islands` 11.89 ms p50 at SP6a's exit, against 14.5; the SP6a spec :1029): its island branch (`src/core/params/presetTrees.ts:197-204`) is a set of non-sky leaves bounded at 4 + SD_MAX from the floor with no `caveBiome{floor}` on their path, so in that tree extension 2's non-sky bound stays 15 and extension 3 does not apply; rule 4's ceiling leaves add the bottom voxels of each run. Expected cost on islands: +0.3-0.6 ms p50 (**estimate**; D25 measures `terrain.islands`). Its lever if it passes the gate: let extension 3 treat the leaves gated by `yAbove{ISLAND_RUN_MIN_BOTTOM}` as a class of their own, so runs below the islands take the band state.

### 5.1 Where the flat floor comes from

### 5.2 The rock floor (F1)

- **The floor height:** `floorY(x, z) = caves.floor.y + caves.floor.amp · |Nf(x, z)|`, Nf the z2 of `caves.noises.floor` (2D, λ 48, 3 octaves) clamped to [−3, 3], a COLUMN-class `noise2` evaluated once per position (`src/gen/density/nodes.ts:309-311`). Defaults: `caves.floor.y` −58, `caves.floor.amp` 5, so floorY runs from −58 (where Nf crosses 0: the gullies) up to −43 (the mounds' tops), about −54 on average (the mean of |z| is about 0.8 for a standard normal z).
- **Why the relief must be in floorY** (the technical review's M2): in the open middle of a wide cavern the cheese slot is between about −16 and −5.8 (16·clamp(…, −1, 1) plus non-negative terms, `src/core/params/defaultTrees.ts:141-145`; the gentle rise `CAP_GENTLE.cheese` is 5.8, `:70`, so the gentle term cannot close a slot below −5.8), and the steep term closes it where S_b = −slot, that is at y between floorY(x, z) + 7 and floorY(x, z) + 7.6. The cave noise adds under one block of relief there, so the floor of every wide cavern has floorY's shape. A smooth floorY (the first draft's ±3 blocks over 100 blocks) would make a nearly flat floor about 19 blocks above the bedrock; the folded noise |Nf| at λ 48 gives mounds 15-30 blocks across with gullies between them.
- **Noise families:** each family cap `G_f·min(1, g_f + …) + 128·min(1, h_f + …)` gains a gentle term `g_b = clamp((floorY(x, z) + 8 + s_b − y) / s_b, 0, 1)` inside its gentle sum, s_b = `caves.floor.span` (8); and a steep term `S_b = 128 · clamp((floorY(x, z) + 8 − y) / 8, 0, 1)` is added at voxel level to the caves slot and the noodle, through the def that today holds the wet roof's `S_w` (`defaultTrees.ts:115-121`; SP6a §1.4): `defs.wetSteep` becomes `defs.steep` = S_w + S_b, so the root's node paths stay as documented at `defaultTrees.ts:250-255` (`root.a.a.b.b` and `root.a.b.b` stay the two refs; the tests that name `defs.wetSteep` follow the rename; alternative: a separate `add` beside each ref, which moves the root paths that `test/unit/inspectorModel.test.ts`, `caveTerms.test.ts`, `fieldSlice.test.ts`, `defaultTrees.test.ts`, `caveTags.test.ts`, `sliceCost.test.ts`, `densityResolve.test.ts`, `test/unit/fixtures/editedTrees.ts` and `test/harness/sp5cTrees.ts` pin). With the family floor F = 48, S_b ≥ 64 > F at y ≤ floorY(x, z) + 4, so no noise-family air lies there.
- **Carvers:** `σ_b = clamp((floorY(x, z) + 8 + s_b − y) / s_b, 0, 1)` joins `σ = max(σ_w, σ_i, σ_u)` (`src/gen/caves/carve.ts:13-17`, `:85-108`), so carvers shrink to nothing at y ≤ floorY + 8; `carvedAt` reads the same noise.
- **The constraint holds over the whole range:** the lowest air voxel is `caves.floor.y` + 5 (noise-family air needs y ≥ floorY(x, z) + 5, and floorY ≥ `caves.floor.y` because |Nf| ≥ 0); it must leave at least two rock voxels above rule 1's dither (top −60), so air ≥ −57, that is `caves.floor.y` ≥ −62. The leaf's range is −62 … −16, so no edit can break it and no lint is needed. At the defaults the lowest air is −53 and carver air −49.
- **The look:** in a wide cavern the floor's air starts at floorY(x, z) + 7: about y −51 in the gullies, −47 on average, up to −36 on the highest mounds; within one cavern the floor's height spreads by about 7-8 blocks between its 10th and 90th percentiles (if z2 is about standard normal: |z| from 0.13 to 1.64, times 5), with slopes of up to about 0.5 block per block on the mounds' flanks, which the 8-block steep part rounds. Smaller caves close rounded at varied heights through the gentle part. The floor block is deepslate (rule 2), in the abyss ash on dark rock (§3.4). D25's checkpoint takes a screenshot of the `bottomCavern` pose and measures `C7.bottomFloorSpread` (§12.3) before the rest is built on the floor.
- **What it costs** (**estimate**, from recon-gen §1.6's histogram: 11.4 % of cave air lies at y −64…−49): about 10-14 % of cave air and about a third of the abyss's. Likely a saving in T: cells with S_b ≥ 64 are decided solid by the bounds without corners (`src/gen/density/bounds.ts:2-23`). D25 measures both.
- **Existing gates the floor moves:** `C4.floorPeak` ≤ 0.1 (`test/thresholds.ts:93`; the largest share one y holds of the floors of components ≥ 10k voxels, `test/harness/caveMetrics.ts:500-523`; 0.014-0.049 today, with the bedrock plane holding its floors at one y; a floor spread over about 15 levels is not expected to raise it) and `C7.flatWallShare` ≤ 0.037 (`test/thresholds.ts:105`; 0.0319 today, a quarter of its flat faces on the y −63 plane, which goes; the mounds' tops add some). Both are measured at D25's generator checkpoint.
- **Alternative** (this spec's decision): a symmetric floor `floorY + amp·Nf` (λ 48, amp 4, `caves.floor.y` −45 to keep the constraint), which gives similar relief without gullies but ends wide caverns about 9 blocks higher on average (more cave air lost); or a 3D lump term in S_b, which costs a voxel-level noise.
- **SP7:** master :751's "everything below y −54 is lava" would never fire at the defaults, because no air lies below −53; SP7 places the abyss's lava from aquifer cells or lowers the floor locally (§16).

## 8. Cave culling (D13, D22, D23)

### 8.1 Data (preallocated, no allocation per frame)

A culler owned by `createStreamingRenderer` (`src/render/regionGrid.ts:254`) sees every event: stage (`:433-461`), reveal (pump step 5, `:566-577`), swap (`:348-354`), retire, remove and unstage (`:342-345`, `:462-481`). The walk is a pure module with no three import, `src/render/culling/caveCulling.ts`, so Node's R3 runs the same code. `onColumnRevealed` (`:578-579`) stays the bench's and the edit-latency probe's.
- `conn: Uint16Array(64·64·24)` on a 64² column torus, slot `((cz & 63) << 6 | (cx & 63))·24 + sy`, with `slotKey: Float64Array(4096)` holding the owning `columnKey` (`src/render/regionGrid.ts:119`). `RD_MAX` 16 (`src/engine/renderDistance.ts:10`): a disc 33 columns wide plus the removal lag fits without collision.
- On a reveal or a swap the culler copies the column's 24 `connectivity15` values from the staged payload (`p.connectivity`, `src/mesh/column.ts:56-57`) and stamps its sections with the walk in progress; on retire it clears the slot key.
- `entry: Uint8Array(98,304)` (faces entered, 6 bits) validated by `entryWalk: Uint32Array`; `queue: Int32Array(98,304)` as a ring holding a section at most once while it waits, new entry bits merging into its waiting mask; a section already popped is pushed again when an entry bit it has not yet been popped with arrives (otherwise a later entry face's exits would be lost). Under the monotone rule a section has at most 3 entry faces, so it is popped at most 3 times; the cost estimate (§8.2) counts pops, not sections. `stamp: Uint32Array(98,304)`, the id of the walk that last reached the section.
- **The draw rule:** a section is drawn when `drawAll` is set or `stamp ≥ shown`, `shown` being the id of the last completed walk. A section reached by the walk in progress passes, so a half-done walk never hides what the previous one showed (no pop-out); a revealed or swapped column draws until a walk that saw its connectivity completes. `drawAll` (no pop-in, §8.2) is set when the last completed walk no longer covers the eye or the world opened a new path, and cleared when a walk that covers both completes.
- Per RegionBatch batch, `sectionOf: Int32Array(maxInstanceCount)` (instance → torus slot), written in `upload` (`src/render/regionBatch.ts:318-333`), regrown in the `'instances'` structural operation (`:291-293`).
- About 1.4 MB of JS heap at RD 16; GPU memory +0.

### 8.2 The walk

- **Start:** the seed box, the 3 × 3 × 3 sections around the camera's section S0 at the walk's start (floating origin plus `camera.position`), each seeded with every face as an exit, even when the eye is inside an opaque voxel in flight.
- **Pop t:** `exits` = the union, over the entered faces f, of the faces g with `connectivityBit(f, g)` set in `conn[t]` (`src/mesh/connectivity.ts:24-30`), computed with at most 5 bit tests per entered face (alternative: a 32,768 × 6-byte exits table, 192 KB).
- **Monotone rule, relative to the box:** step +x only if `sx ≥ S0x − 1`, −x only if `sx ≤ S0x + 1`; likewise y and z.
- **Why it misses nothing a ray sees from any eye in the box:** a 3D DDA ray from an eye in section e visits a 6-connected chain of non-opaque voxels until it stops; its section coordinates move monotonically away from e on each axis, and e lies within one section of S0, so every step it takes is allowed by the rule; e is seeded with every face an exit; each section it crosses joins its entry face to its exit face through those voxels, so the connectivity bit is set (opaque = PASS opaque with `fullFaces` 63, `src/mesh/connectivity.ts:69`); the section where it stops is entered, so it is stamped. One visit per section would lose a later entry face with different exits, so entry sets are kept. A completed walk therefore stays valid for every eye inside its box.
- **Neighbours:** a column within RD of the camera that is not revealed (streaming, after a teleport, while an Apply refills) is treated as fully open (connectivity 0x7FFF in every section) and stepped through, because it draws nothing and the player sees through it; only a column beyond RD (not in the torus) stops the walk. A newly revealed column can only close paths, so a reveal never sets `drawAll`. Nothing above y 319 or below y −64 is stepped into; an eye outside −64 … 319 (flight has no altitude clamp, `src/player/fly.ts`) sets `drawAll` and starts no walk.
- **Triggers and `drawAll`:** (1) the camera's section differs from S0: a new walk starts (after the one in progress completes); the old set stays in force while the camera's section is inside the old box, and `drawAll` is set if it leaves the box before a walk seeded around it completes (a teleport, or flight faster than one section per walk). (2) A swap (an edit or an Apply) whose new `connectivity15` has a bit the old one lacks in any section (a wall dug open): `drawAll` is set and a walk starts; a swap that only removes bits keeps the set (it was a superset). (3) Reveals: after reveals, a walk starts when none is running, at most once every 250 ms while the reveal queue is not empty and once when it drains, so a disc streamed in while the camera stands still is culled (§8.1's rule keeps new columns drawn until then). One walk at a time; dirt during a walk restarts it after it completes. `drawAll` is cleared when a walk completes whose box holds the camera's section and that started after the last trigger that set it.
- **Budget:** time-sliced at 0.5 ms per frame, `performance.now()` polled every 256 pops (the master's 1.5 ms is lowered because `live`'s main p95 is 5.06 ms against the 6 ms gate). A full walk at RD 12 (11,736 sections, up to 3 pops each) is 0.3-1.5 ms, at RD 16 (about 21,400) 0.5-2.5 ms (**estimate**, the box start adds a few per cent; the bench row `cull.walk` measures it).
- **Off:** no walk while the X-ray is on (its pass is never culled and the terrain passes draw nothing, D24), during `warmUp` (`src/render/renderer.ts:763-765`) and before the reveal frame; `drawAll` is then set so everything draws.
- **Alternative** (this spec's decision; the builder check's question 2): a single-section seed with `drawAll` set on every camera section change until the next walk completes, which is simpler but draws everything for 1-5 frames at every section boundary.

## 10. Failure modes and review focus

- **A seam at chunk borders** (two columns reading a shared position differently): `readFieldHalo` and its two-column unit test (§1.2); `B3.borderLattice`.
