## Goal

Q turns on the selection tool. With it, LMB and RMB mark two corners on the aimed blocks, up to 32 blocks away, and a yellow box with its size shows the volume, through terrain too. Ctrl+C copies it; Ctrl+X cuts it (the volume empties); N moves it (the place it leaves goes back to the terrain); Ctrl+V pastes the clipboard. A paste or a move shows a translucent ghost of the blocks that follows the aim, resting on the aimed surface (the motivating case: a house a terrain change left floating or buried, put back on the ground), with the blocks it would replace in red and the count of blocks it changes; the mouse wheel turns it 90°, ↑ and ↓ raise and lower it, RMB pastes, LMB cancels. Logs, ladders and wall torches turn with it (`rotateState`). Each cut, paste and move is one undo step, persisted with the world (SP5b). The clipboard is one per browser, kept in IndexedDB `wi10`'s `meta` store: it survives reloads, terrain changes and switching worlds. Underneath: the sim's validation becomes linear and a large batch is applied in sub-batches of 512 edits. The target for a paste of 32,768 changed blocks is to be visible within 2 s with no frame over 33 ms; Node measured 0.4 s for solid blocks, and about 1.5 s is estimated for a build full of glass and torches (§3.5); Chrome is measured by bench leg L12. A `copy` message reads volumes in the sim; the history is capped by its total edits. The three SP5b notes are done: a Save button for Rename, a notice when Modify terrain is switched on, and the reminder "Enable 'Modify terrain' to edit here" on the map's disabled parameters. Measured by L3, E3 and E5 parts for pastes (§10.1), the `paste.max` and `paste.maxLit` bench rows and bench leg L12 with its gate `G2.paste` (§10.2-§10.4). The generated world does not change (`GENERATOR_VERSION` 5, the 59 goldens byte-identical).

## Decisions taken by this spec (for the user's review)

### Decisions with a visible effect

- **D1 The selection tool is a mode, switched by Q** (§1.1). *You notice:* Q turns the tool on and off, with the world owning the controls. While it is on, the build HUD shows a label above the hotbar (D3) and the mouse buttons belong to the tool (D2); 1-9, the wheel, E, F, M, B, G, H and the movement keys work as before. Turning it off hides the box and cancels a paste or move in progress (the clipboard is kept; a cancelled move changed nothing); the corners are kept for the page's life and show again at the next Q. The selection is not saved: a reload starts with none. Q is free (`src/player/keymap.ts:48-78`) and reachable with the left hand on WASD. Alternative: a hotbar item (a "wand" slot), which needs a new `Slot` kind through `normalizeSlot` (`src/player/hotbar.ts:69`), the palette's Fluids group (`src/ui/palette.ts:38`) and MMB's pick (`hotbar.ts:139`).
- **D2 Marking the corners** (§1.2). *You notice:* with the tool on, LMB marks corner 1 and RMB corner 2 at the aimed block; the black outline shows which. The tool's ray reaches 32 blocks, walking or flying (the editing reach stays 6 and 10 with the tool off, `src/player/raycast.ts:26-27`). A press acts once: "Hold mouse button to repeat" does not apply to the tool. MMB does nothing. A press that aims at nothing (the sky, or beyond 32 blocks) changes nothing. Alternative: the editing reach (6 walking, 10 flying), which makes a 64-block selection a walk between its corners.
- **D3 The box and the label** (§1.3, §6.1). *You notice:* the box around both corners in yellow, its hidden edges dimmed through terrain; with one corner, a one-block box there. The label: "Selection tool: LMB marks corner 1, RMB corner 2" with no corner; "Corner 1 at x, y, z: RMB marks corner 2" (or the reverse) with one; "Selection W × H × D (n blocks) · Ctrl+C copy · Ctrl+X cut · N move · Ctrl+V paste" with both, where W, H and D are the sizes along x, y and z and the key names come from the key map (`bindingLabel`, `keymap.ts:141`) so SP10's remapping renames them; with a side over 64 the box turns red and the label reads "Selection W × H × D: at most 64 blocks on a side". Alternative: the size only in the F3 HUD (`src/ui/hud.ts:115-145`), which is hidden by default.
- **D4 The keys** (§1.1). *You notice:* Q (selection tool), Ctrl+C (copy), Ctrl+X (cut), Ctrl+V (paste), N (move), and while a ghost is shown: the wheel (down turns it 90° clockwise, up turns it back), ↑ and ↓ (raise and lower it one block). They are in a new "Selection tool" group of the help (H), with two loading-screen tips. They join `DEFAULT_KEYMAP`, so the help, the tips and SP10's remapping see them; the action count goes from 29 to 38.
  - Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V are code bindings that match only with Ctrl (or Cmd) held and Alt not held, so AltGr+C on the user's Spanish layout (which sets both Ctrl and Alt) never copies, as `shortcutFor` already refuses Alt (`src/ui/common/keys.ts:41-42`). Q, N and the arrows match only without Ctrl.
  - **Only while placing:** the wheel turns and the arrows raise and lower; otherwise the wheel selects hotbar slots as today and the arrows are not claimed, so the page does not prevent their default (today every resolved key is prevented, `src/player/input.ts:121-125`). The turn is on the wheel, not on a key, so a turn after Ctrl+V reaches no browser chord: with Ctrl still held, the wheel turns while walking (walking reads Ctrl+wheel as the plain wheel) and changes the flying speed while flying, as today. Tab stays free for the page's buttons.
  - **Browser chords that stay the browser's:** Ctrl+N (a new window; in fullscreen Keyboard Lock hands it to the page, `src/ui/world/worldPage.ts:1848`, and it does nothing), Ctrl+Q (Firefox on Linux asks to quit) and Ctrl+Tab. A user who keeps Ctrl down after Ctrl+C and presses N or Q reaches them. The tool's follow-up actions after Ctrl+V (turn, raise, lower, RMB, LMB) need no letter, so the common sequence never does.
  - B, G, R and T keep their meaning; Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y keep theirs by context (`keys.ts:41-54`). With focus in a text field or the map owning the controls, none of them acts, so the map's fields keep the browser's copy and paste (`input.ts:118-131`). ↑ and ↓ act once per press: holding them does not repeat (`input.ts:129`, `!e.repeat`).
  - Alternative: Tab turns (Ctrl+Tab then switches the browser tab when Ctrl is still held from Ctrl+V); or copy, cut and paste as shortcuts outside the key map, as Ctrl+Z is (`keys.ts:14`), with a fixed help line like `UNDO_REDO_LINE` (`src/ui/help.ts:50`) and no remapping.
- **D5 Copy** (§2.1). *You notice:* Ctrl+C with both corners set copies the selected blocks, air and fluids included, into the clipboard, replacing it: "Copied W × H × D" (1.5 s). Ctrl+C with fewer than two corners: "Mark two corners first: Q, then LMB and RMB" (4 s). A selection with a side over 64 is refused with D3's text. A selection that reaches a column that is not loaded is refused: "Part of the selection is not loaded: come closer and try again" (4 s). A copy works while the world regenerates: it copies what the world shows then (a stale column is still Published). Alternative: copy the loaded part and mark the rest as "no block" (a mask in the clipboard).
- **D6 Cut** (§2.2). *You notice:* Ctrl+X copies as D5 does, then empties the volume: every block becomes air and every fluid goes (the clipboard holds them), as one undo step: "Cut W × H × D" (1.5 s). Every check the page can make (the 32,768 cap, the loaded and regenerating gate) runs before the clipboard is written, so a cut refused by them leaves the previous clipboard as it was: "Can't cut: <reason>" (4 s). The volume empties only after the clipboard is safely stored (D26), so a reload right after a cut always finds the cut blocks in the clipboard; if storing fails, nothing is cut: "Could not keep the clipboard (<reason>): nothing was cut" (6 s). Only the sim's own refusals (the blocks changed, memory full) can follow the write: then nothing is cut and the clipboard holds the selection, as after Ctrl+C. Alternative: a cut ladder or other `hold` block leaves its water, as LMB does (`src/player/interaction.ts:85-93`).
- **D7 Paste** (§2.3, §2.5). *You notice:* Ctrl+V turns the tool on if it is off and shows the clipboard's ghost (D11), unturned, following the aim: it rests on a top face, hangs under a bottom face and stands against a side face, centred on the aimed spot (§2.5's rule). The wheel turns it 90° (down clockwise seen from above, up back); ↑ and ↓ move it up and down one block (the offset lasts until the paste ends). Where a paste cannot go is known from the ghost's position alone (it would leave the world's height or its edge, or part of it is not loaded), the ghost's box turns red at once with that reason in the label. The counts of blocks it changes and replaces are read once the ghost has stood still for 100 ms, so sweeping the aim reads nothing. RMB pastes it, as one undo step: "Pasted W × H × D" (1.5 s); the selection becomes the pasted volume and the tool stays on with no ghost, so N moves it again at once. LMB or Q cancels. Aiming at nothing leaves the ghost where it was; with no aim since Ctrl+V the ghost is hidden and RMB does nothing. A paste the world cannot take is refused with its reason (D13), the ghost's box red beforehand where the reason is known. Ctrl+V with no clipboard: "The clipboard is empty: select a volume (Q) and copy it (Ctrl+C)" (4 s). Alternative: LMB pastes and RMB cancels; or the ghost stays after a paste for further copies.
- **D8 Move** (§2.4). *You notice:* N with both corners set shows the selection's ghost as Ctrl+V does, while the blocks stay where they are; RMB moves them: the new volume fills and the part of the old volume the new one does not cover goes back to the generated terrain, as one undo step ("Moved W × H × D", 1.5 s), so one Ctrl+Z puts the house back where it was. So a house left floating, moved down, leaves air where it was (the terrain there is air), and a house left buried, moved up, leaves the new ground, not a house-sized hole. The source and the target may overlap (moving a house down 3 blocks). The clipboard is not changed. LMB or Q cancels and nothing has changed. Alternative: the old volume becomes air, as a cut does (a buried house moved up then leaves a hole); or no move key, a move being Ctrl+X then Ctrl+V, two undo steps (Point 1's alternative).
- **D9 Air and fluids in a paste** (§2.3). *You notice:* a paste writes the whole volume as copied: the clipboard's air replaces whatever is inside the ghost's box (terrain bumps inside a pasted house are cleared, and a box pasted into a slope cuts its notch). Fluids are pasted as copied (a source stays a source, a level byte stays as it was; nothing flows until SP7), and a waterlogged ladder keeps its water. The ghost shows the blocks, not the fluids. Alternative: skip the clipboard's air (a mask: only its blocks are written, so the terrain inside a house stays), or a key that switches between the two.
- **D10 Rotation** (§2.6). *You notice:* the wheel turns the ghost 90° clockwise seen from above (down) or back (up), four steps a full turn; the blocks turn with it: a log's axis swaps x and z, a ladder and a wall torch keep their wall, a standing torch is unchanged. No mirroring and no turns about x or z. Alternative: mirroring with `mirrorState` and turns about x and z (a `rotateState` about y alone cannot turn a log's axis into y).
- **D11 The ghost** (§6.2, §6.3). *You notice:* the clipboard's blocks, translucent (alpha 0.45), lit as open sky but never darker than 60 % of full daylight, so it stays visible at night; at the paste's place and turn, each face shaded by the direction it faces after the turn, glass showing its frame; its box in cyan, hidden edges dimmed through terrain, red when the paste would be refused; the blocks the paste would replace (any block or fluid inside the box that the paste changes) as red translucent cubes seen through terrain, at most 4,096 of them drawn; and the label "Paste W × H × D · turned 90° · n blocks change, m replaced · RMB pastes, LMB cancels · Wheel turns · ↑ ↓ raise or lower" (the counts read "counting…" until the target is read, §2.5), or "Move …" for a move. A large clipboard's ghost builds over a few frames; its box shows at once. A ghost with more than 131,072 faces shows its box only, with "no preview: too many faces" in the label. Alternative: the box alone, with no blocks and no replaced cells.
- **D12 The bounds** (§2.7). *You notice:* a selection may be up to 64 blocks on each side (262,144 blocks); one cut, paste or move may change at most 32,768 blocks, counted after leaving out blocks that would not change (a move counts both volumes). A paste of a house (a 32 × 12 × 32 shell changes about 3,300 blocks) is far inside it; a solid 64³ hillside can be copied but not pasted whole ("it would change 262,144 blocks; at most 32,768 at once"). A paste of that maximum shows in about 0.5 s for solid blocks (measured in Node, §3.5) and, by an estimate not yet measured, about 1.5 s for a build full of glass and torches, with "Working on n blocks…" meanwhile. The 32,768 cap is confirmed or lowered by D31's prototype before any code uses it. Alternative: 32 blocks a side and 16,384 changes (about half the wait, and every selection pastes whole).
- **D13 Refusals happen before anything is written** (§2.7). *You notice:* "Can't paste here: <reason>" (or "Can't move here: …"), 4 s, nothing changed, the ghost kept: "it would leave the world (y −64 … 319)", "it would cross the world's edge", "part of it is not loaded", "the world is regenerating", "you are inside it", "it would change n blocks; at most 32,768 at once", "the blocks changed; try again". A paste is refused, never clipped, so a build is never cut silently. A pasted block may not overlap your body, as a placed block may not (master §4.12). Alternative: clip a paste at the world's limits; or paste over your body and let the unstuck scan lift you (SP5a's rule, master §4.17).
- **D14 Undo and redo of whole volumes** (§5). *You notice:* Ctrl+Z after a cut, paste or move takes the whole volume back, Ctrl+Y puts it again, after a reload too (SP5b D6). The history keeps its 100 steps per side and, new, at most 65,536 changed blocks in all: a new step drops the oldest steps until it fits (the newest step is always kept whole). So two maximum operations fill it: after them, any later step, even a single block, drops the older of the two from undo, with every step older than it. Alternative: a total of 98,304 (three maximum operations; about 4.4 MB per history write instead of 2.9 MB); or per-step history records (a `wi10` version bump), which lift the total; it is D28's lever 3.
- **D15 One clipboard per browser** (§4). *You notice:* what you copy in one world pastes in any other, in this browser; it survives reloads, terrain changes, the backup copy, renaming and deleting worlds, and the start screen. Two tabs share it: the last copy or cut wins, and Ctrl+V reads the kept one each time. A clipboard copied by a later version of the app whose block types this one does not know pastes them as air with "n blocks of unknown types became air" (master §2.2's rule; PER-3 applies to it too). It is not in a `.wiworld`. In a browser window that cannot store data, the clipboard lasts until the page closes ("The clipboard is kept only until this page closes: this browser window cannot store data", 6 s, at the first copy). Alternative: one clipboard per world, travelling in its export and its backup copy, with no pasting across worlds.
- **D16 The tool and the rest of the world** (§2.8). *You notice:* pasting, cutting and moving are block edits: they work with Modify terrain off or on, never make a backup copy, and are refused while the world regenerates (D13); copying is not (D5). Leaving the world (Load world, Back) right after a large paste waits for it, showing "Working on n blocks…", so the paste is saved before the page goes. Saved places, the map, teleporting, walking and flying are unchanged; the tool works walking or flying, and its box and ghost hide while the map, the help, the palette, the B dialog or Settings is open, as the outline does (`worldPage.ts:4049`).
- **D17 Rename's Save button** (§7.1, UX note 1). *You notice:* while a name is being edited in Load world, a "Save" button beside the field keeps it, as Enter does; Esc still cancels. Alternative: an icon (✓) in place of the word; or Save and Cancel buttons.
- **D18 The Modify terrain notice** (§7.2, UX note 2). *You notice:* each time Modify terrain is switched on: "Modify terrain is on: the first terrain change saves a backup copy of this world first, if you have built in it." (6 s), in the map's notice row. Alternative: two texts by the world's state (one for a world with builds, one without).
- **D19 The reminder on disabled parameters** (§7.3, UX note 3). *You notice:* with Modify terrain off, hovering any terrain control of the map (a parameter row, the seed, the preset, the biome size, the biome table, the spline and density editors, the Presets import, the map's Undo and Redo) shows "Enable 'Modify terrain' to edit here"; a parameter fixed by a rule keeps "fixed: …". Alternative: one reminder per editor, on its tab, not per control.

### Technical decisions (no visible effect)

- **D20 Linear validation** (§3.1). `validate` (`src/sim/edits.ts:250-269`) scans back over every earlier edit of the batch for the same voxel (`:259-263`), which is quadratic: measured 12 ms at 4,096 edits, 225 ms at 16,384 and 810-880 ms at 32,768, 61-66 % of that batch. A `Map` from the voxel's packed position to its in-batch value gives the same answer in linear time, built only for a batch of more than one edit. Alternative: none at a 32,768 cap (without it the cap would be 4,096).
- **D21 Sub-batches of 512 in the sim, with a headroom check** (§3.2). `applyEdits` validates the whole batch first. A batch of more than 512 edits then checks the store's headroom (each pool's free slots plus the slots it can still grow by) against one sub-batch's worst case, and refuses `full` with nothing written when it is short. It then applies the batch 512 edits at a time, ending its read section after each (publish, `exitRead`, `enterRead`), so the retired slots are reclaimed between sub-batches; the reply stays one `edited` message, one history step, one `markEdited`. A `StoreFull` in sub-batch k (another thread took the headroom meanwhile) rolls k back and writes the earlier sub-batches' previous values back in sub-batches of its own (a compensating pass), so the batch stays all or nothing. Measured in Node (recon §11): a 32,768-edit solid paste as one batch takes 1,312-1,334 ms and grows the shared pools, from a fresh region, by 256 + 135 MiB, which never shrink (the pools only grow, `src/world/store/pool.ts:10-12`); in sub-batches of 1,024, 411 ms in all, at most 70 ms per sub-batch, at most 3,979 slots waiting for reclamation, and no growth on a pool that had already grown (growth from a fresh pool was not measured; L3.paste records it, §10.1). The slots in limbo depend on the paste's order (SP5c Task 3, Node, one placement each): at 1,024 a maximum paste written y descending left 6,719 (solid) and 9,925 (`paste.maxLit`'s lit shells), y ascending 4,205 and 4,441, so no order kept §10.5's bound of 4,096; at 512, y ascending leaves 2,198 and 2,536 (its undo the same), `editMs` unchanged within 1-2 % and the longest sub-batch halved (70-77 ms). So the size is 512 (D28's lever 2, taken as the design) and the paste is written y ascending (§2.3). Alternative: the page splits the batch into messages and posts the inverse of the accepted ones on a refusal (several replies and remesh rounds, a merged step). It would break SP5a §10's "a paste is one `edits` message", which is why it is not chosen.
- **D22 The sim reads volumes** (§3.3). A new `copy` message reads a box of at most 64 per side in the sim worker and replies with its values, transferred, and for a move's source also the generated values (D8). A box outside the world's height or window is answered as outside, not as a malformed message, so a ghost aimed past the world's edge never ends editing. Copy, cut, move and the paste's target all read through it, so a read is consistent with every edit before it (the sim answers messages in order, `src/workers/simHandler.ts:1-17`) and the main thread never reads 262,144 voxels in a frame (about 23 ms at 88 ns each, measured). Alternative: main-thread reads (`blockAt`, `fluidAt`) sliced over frames, with a version check for edits made between slices.
- **D23 Bulk batches on the edit path** (§3.4). A batch of more than 2 edits (a door's two halves are the largest click, master §4.12) opens no latency sample, so the HUD's "edit p95" and the bench's G2 stay single-edit figures (today every accepted batch opens one, `src/ui/world/editPath.ts:103`), and shows "Working on n blocks…" while its reply is pending past 250 ms. While a bulk batch is pending, the autosave's tick and a navigation's flush wait for its reply up to `BULK_SETTLE_WAIT_MS` = 5,000 ms (today the tick waits at most 1,000 ms and a navigation does not wait, `src/ui/world/autosave.ts:33`, `:252-296`), so the flush that follows a paste carries its step, and a leave does not kill the sim before the paste is saved. Alternative: a flag passed by the tool (undo and redo of a large step would then need it too).
- **D24 `ClipboardV1` by state keys** (§4.1). The stored clipboard holds a table of the state keys it uses and, per voxel, an index into it, so it reads under any later registry, by key, as saves do (master §2.2; SP5b D22). Alternative: global state ids with a full `stateTable`, as a world record.
- **D25 The ghost is meshed on the main thread, sliced** (§6.2). ## 2. Copy, cut, paste and move

### 2.1 Copy (D5, D22)

The selection's box is `min(corner1, corner2)` … `max(corner1, corner2)` per axis, inclusive. Ctrl+C checks the sides (≤ 64), posts `copy` (§3.3) and, on `copied` with values, builds the clipboard (§4.1), keeps it in memory and writes it (§4.2); on `copied` with null (a column not Published), D5's refusal. The clipboard is unturned: the copy's own orientation.

### 2.2 Cut (D6, D26)

In this order:
1. The sides checked (≤ 64) and `copy` of the box posted (§3.3); on `copied` null, D5's refusal.
2. The batch built from the read values: for every voxel of the box whose value (`state << 8 | fluid`) is not 0, `{op: 'set', value: 0, expect: its read value}`.
3. The page's checks, before anything is written: at most 32,768 edits (D12, else "Can't cut: it would change n blocks; at most 32,768 at once"), and the edit path's gate over every edited column's 3 × 3 (`editState`, `src/ui/world/editPath.ts:84-94`: "part of it is not loaded", "the world is regenerating"). A refusal here leaves the previous clipboard as it was.
4. The clipboard built (§4.1), kept in memory and written (§4.2); the write awaited (`complete`, or memory without storage). A failed write cuts nothing (D6).
5. The batch posted through `createActionPost` (`src/ui/world/blockHistory.ts:174-184`) as one step. Only the sim's refusals (`'changed'`, `'full'`) can come after step 4; they leave the box as it was and the clipboard holding the selection.

### 2.3 Paste (D7, D9, D12, D13)

At RMB: the target box from the anchor (§2.5); the immediate checks (bounds and the body, D13); `copy` of the target box; then `pasteBatch.ts` builds, for every cell of the turned clipboard, `value = turned(c)` and, where it differs from the target's read value, `{op: 'set', value, expect: read}` (a `set` equal to the generated value removes the DiffStore entry, `src/sim/edits.ts:386-389`, so a paste that restores terrain leaves no entry); refuses over 32,768; and posts through `createActionPost` as one step. The batch's order is y ascending, then z, then x (the room before the roof over it). Not y descending (a roof written before the room under it), because that order breaks §3.2's limbo bound: each cell of a layer darkens the light sections under it, which are re-written once per edit, and their retired slots wait in limbo until the sub-batch's drain; measured in sub-batches of 512 (SP5c Task 3, Node), a maximum paste written y descending leaves 3,692 slots in limbo (solid) and 6,324 (`paste.maxLit`'s content), y ascending 2,198 and 2,536, against the bound of 4,096 (§10.5). Each relight's cost also depends on the order (recon §11, "Placement order matters for relight-heavy content"), so D31's prototype times both orders once on `paste.maxLit` and records them; it keeps y descending only if that order is faster and its `limboMax` is at most 4,096 on both rows, and y ascending otherwise. The clipboard's air is a value like any other (D9). The `expect` values catch any change between the read and the apply (the sim refuses the batch as `'changed'`, D13).

### 2.4 Move (D8)

At N: `copy` of the selection; the ghost shows its values (the clipboard is not touched). At RMB: `copy` of the source box with its generated values (`gen: true`, §3.3) and of the target box (two messages, answered in order); the batch over the union of both boxes, one edit per voxel:
- a target cell takes the turned source value read now: `{op: 'set', value, expect: read}` where it differs from the read value;
- a source cell outside the target goes back to the generated terrain: `{op: 'revert', value: 0, expect: read}` where its generated value differs from its read value (a `revert` removes the cell's DiffStore entry, `edits.ts:385-388`). Above the ground the generated value is air, so a floating house leaves air; inside new ground it is that ground, so a buried house leaves no hole.

One voxel appears once, so the sim's in-batch rule (an earlier edit of the voxel counts as written, `edits.ts:259-262`) is never needed for a move. At most 32,768 edits (D12), counted after leaving out the cells that do not change.

### 2.5 The ghost's anchor (D7, D11)

Let the turned sizes be (W′, H, D′) and the aim's hit voxel h with face normal n, and a = h + n (`across`, `src/player/interaction.ts:97-104`, exported for this). The box's minimum corner:
- n = +y: (a.x − ⌊W′/2⌋, a.y, a.z − ⌊D′/2⌋); n = −y: (a.x − ⌊W′/2⌋, a.y − H + 1, a.z − ⌊D′/2⌋);
- n = +x: (a.x, a.y, a.z − ⌊D′/2⌋); n = −x: (a.x − W′ + 1, a.y, a.z − ⌊D′/2⌋);
- n = +z: (a.x − ⌊W′/2⌋, a.y, a.z); n = −z: (a.x − ⌊W′/2⌋, a.y, a.z − D′ + 1);
then y + `offset`. The ghost never covers the aimed block.
- **At each anchor, turn or offset change, on the main thread, with no read:** §2.7's y and window checks over the target box, and `editState` over the 3 × 3 of every column the box covers (at most 7 × 7 columns for a 64-wide box). A box that fails turns red with its reason ("it would leave the world (y −64 … 319)", "it would cross the world's edge", "part of it is not loaded", "the world is regenerating"), and no `copy` is posted for it.
- **The target read, throttled:** once the anchor, the turn and the offset have stayed the same for 100 ms (`TARGET_READ_SETTLE_MS`), the page posts `copy` of the target box (one in flight; a newer box replaces a queued one). On its reply it counts the changed cells n and the replaced cells m (changed cells whose target value is not 0) and fills the replaced-cell overlay with the first 4,096 in x, z, y order (§6.3). A reply for a box that is no longer the ghost's is dropped. A `copied` with `why: 'unloaded'` marks the target "part of it is not loaded" (the box red). Until a reply, the counts read "counting…". So a sweeping aim reads nothing, and a still ghost is read once.

### 2.6 Rotation (D10)

One turn is clockwise seen from +y (north (0, −1) → east (1, 0)), the sense of `REGISTRY.rotateState` (`src/world/blocks/registry.ts:113`, `:372-377`; master §2.2). Inside a box of W (x) by D (z) a cell (x, y, z) goes to (D − 1 − z, y, x) and the sizes become D by W; a value `s << 8 | f` goes to `rotateState(s, 1) << 8 | f` (the fluid byte has no direction, `src/world/blocks/fluid.ts:2-4`). q turns apply this q times; `clipboard.ts` precomputes nothing per frame: the batch builder turns positions arithmetically and states by the registry's table (a lookup, `registry.ts:372-377`). Today's oriented states are `oak_log`'s axis, `wall_torch`'s and `ladder`'s facing (`src/world/blocks/defs.ts:79`, `:93`, `:98`); `provisionalBoxes.ts:40-43` builds their boxes with `rotateState`, so the turned box and the turned state agree.

### 2.7 Bounds and refusals (D12, D13)

| check | when | value | reason text |
|---|---|---|---|
| a selection side | at Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X, N | ≤ 64 | D3's text |
| the clipboard | at copy | ≤ 64 a side, so ≤ 262,144 cells, ≤ 768 KiB stored | — |
| changed blocks per batch | before posting a cut, paste, move | ≤ 32,768 (`MAX_STEP_EDITS`) | it would change n blocks; at most 32,768 at once |
| y range | at each ghost position (before any `copy`) and at RMB | every target cell in −64 … 319 (`src/core/coords.ts:36`) | it would leave the world (y −64 … 319) |
| the window | at each ghost position (before any `copy`) and at RMB | x, z in [−524,288, 524,288) (`coords.ts:23`, `:71`) | it would cross the world's edge |
| loaded | at each ghost position (`editState` over the box's columns' 3 × 3s), the `copy` (`why: 'unloaded'`) and the edit path's gate | every column Published, every edited column's 3 × 3 at `editState` 0 (`editPath.ts:84-94`) | part of it is not loaded |
| regenerating | at each ghost position and the edit path's gate | no stale column (`editState` 2) | the world is regenerating |
| the body | at RMB | no colliding cell (`COLLIDE` not none) of the turned clipboard intersects the player's box, walking or flying, as `placeAt`'s rule (`interaction.ts:143-157`) | you are inside it |
| changed since read | the sim's `'changed'` | — | the blocks changed; try again |

An edit outside y or the window would make the sim refuse the `edits` message as BAD_MESSAGE, which latches the handler and ends editing for the session (`src/workers/protocol.ts:780-782`; `src/workers/simHandler.ts:254-256`; `src/engine/simClient.ts:15-20`), so these checks run before posting; the sim's guard keeps its own check and gains the length cap (§3.3) as a backstop. A `copy` outside them is answered `why: 'outside'`, not BAD_MESSAGE (§3.3), and the page never posts one (the checks above run first). A refused paste keeps the ghost; `full` keeps SP5a's "Could not edit: memory full" (`editPath.ts:54`). The selection's own box never fails y or the window: its corners are aimed blocks.

### 2.8 The modification mode, places, movement and leaving (D16, D23)

- A paste is a block edit: no `beforeApply` path is involved and no backup is made (SP5b §3.6). During an Apply the gate refuses it (`editPath.ts:91-94`). A copy is allowed while the world regenerates (D5).
- **The autosave tick.** Today it waits for the edit path to settle at most `SETTLE_WAIT_MS` = 1,000 ms (`src/ui/world/autosave.ts:33`, `:252-269`), then posts; the sim answers in order, so a flush posted during a 1.5 s paste writes the paste's DiffStore columns but not its step, and a crash in the next 2 s would leave a paste with no undo step. New: while `editPath.bulkPending()` (§3.4), the wait is `BULK_SETTLE_WAIT_MS` = 5,000 ms, so the flush after a paste carries its step.
- **Leaving.** Today `beforeLeave` posts at once and waits at most `LEAVE_WAIT_MS` = 1,000 ms (`autosave.ts:35`, `:285-296`), and `leaveAfterSave` navigates when it times out (`src/ui/world/worldPage.ts:2193`, `r === null`). A flush posted behind a 1.5 s paste times out, the page navigates, and the worker dies with the paste unsaved. New: `beforeLeave` first waits for the edit path to settle, up to `BULK_SETTLE_WAIT_MS` while a bulk batch is pending (with "Working on n blocks…" showing), then posts and waits `LEAVE_WAIT_MS` as today. The tab's own close (`pagehide`) stays the accepted crash window of SP5b.

## 3. The engine

### 3.1 Linear validation (D20)

`validate` keeps a `Map<number, number>` from `colKey · 98,304 + (y + 64) · 256 + lz · 16 + lx` (below 2^53) to the batch's value so far: an edit's current value is the map's entry (a `revert`'s generated value, or the earlier edit's `value`), else the store's (`edits.ts:264`); the edit then writes its own. The refusal order and reasons are unchanged (`'unloaded'`, `'changed'`, `'noProto'`, `edits.ts:250-269`). A single-edit batch builds no map.

### 3.2 Sub-batches (D21)

- `SUB_BATCH = 512` (not the recon's 1,024, whose maximum paste leaves more than 4,096 slots in limbo in either order, D21). After validation, `applyEdits` (`edits.ts:296-449`) runs the per-edit loop (`:327-391`) as today and, after every 512 edits, publishes the opened writers (`:392`), calls `exitRead` and `enterRead` (`:306`, `:427`) and reopens writers as needed. Each sub-batch's retired slots are reclaimed at its `exitRead` (`src/world/store/reclaim.ts:13-21`). `EditResult` gains `limboMax`, the most entries in the sim thread's limbo at the end of any sub-batch, before its `exitRead` (the reclaim buffer's `[R_LIMBO + t]`, `reclaim.ts:9`, `:33`; 0 for a refusal before any write), and `subBatchMaxMs`, the longest sub-batch. L12 records both per operation (§10.4), and they reach the page only through the reply, so `EditedMsg` carries them too: `parseFromSim`'s `edited` case (`src/workers/protocol.ts:871-877`) requires each finite and ≥ 0, and the handler copies them from the result (`src/workers/simHandler.ts:428-433`).
- **The drain needs read depth 0.** `exitRead` drains only when it closes the outermost read section (`reclaim.ts:182`, `if (--depth > 0) return;`), so `applyEdits` is never called inside a read section: the sim handler calls it at depth 0 (`simHandler.ts:428`), and so does every test, metric and bench row that reads `limboMax`. Called inside one, the batch is still applied whole and exact, but nothing is reclaimed until the caller's own `exitRead`, and `limboMax` counts every retire of the batch.
- **Why the headroom check.** Every `replaceBlocks` and `replaceLight` stores into a new slot (copy-on-write, `src/world/store/store.ts:561-571`, `:582-595`), and the intermediate slots it retires wait in limbo while the sim is in its read section (`reclaim.ts:13-21`). A rollback frees nothing until the next drain. So a `StoreFull` in sub-batch k, followed by a compensating pass in the same read section, would most likely throw a second `StoreFull`, which is the sim's failure.
- **The headroom check.** A batch of more than `SUB_BATCH` edits, after validation and before any write, reads each pool's headroom: `freeCount() + maxSlots − slotCount()` (`src/world/store/pool.ts:90`, `:103-104`; the store exposes `blockPool` and `bytePool`, `store.ts:94-95`). It refuses `full` with nothing written when the block pool's headroom is below `HEADROOM_BLOCK_SLOTS` = 4,096 or the byte pool's below `HEADROOM_BYTE_SLOTS` = 8,192 (above one sub-batch's measured worst case, a maximum paste of `paste.maxLit`'s content written y ascending, or its undo: 512 block slots and 2,024 byte slots in limbo, 2,536 in all; SP5c Task 3, Node). The page's existing notice then applies: "Could not edit: memory full" (`editPath.ts:54`).
- **A `StoreFull` in sub-batch k** (another thread took the headroom after the check): the rollback undoes sub-batch k (`edits.ts:393-418`), with its loops limited to sub-batch k's edits (today's restore the DiffStore entries of every recorded edit, `:403-411`, which would undo the published sub-batches' entries before the compensating pass runs). Then `exitRead` and `enterRead` drain the rolled-back slots, and a compensating pass writes back, in reverse order and in sub-batches of `SUB_BATCH` with a drain after each, the previous voxel value of every edit of sub-batches 0 … k−1 (the value each validated edit replaced, kept from validation in a `Uint32Array`), with the same relight. The compensating pass sets each DiffStore entry from `prev[]` directly, as the rollback does (`:403-411`), not by the per-edit rule (`:385-388`), so an entry that equalled the generated value before the batch (possible after an Apply) is restored, not removed. The column's diff flag (`setDiffFlag`) stays set, as after any edit. Values, light and the DiffStore then equal the batch's start. The reply is `refused: 'full'` with `dirty` covering every column touched, so they remesh. A throw inside the compensating pass is the sim's failure (INTERNAL; `simClient.ts:15-20`): editing stops with SP5b D14's notice and at most the last 2 s are lost, the last resort.
- No other message runs between sub-batches (the sim answers each message in order and synchronously, `simHandler.ts:4`, `:411-457`): a D job's diff read-through, a flush, a target `copy` and every click wait for the whole batch, up to its whole `editMs` (about 0.4-1.5 s at the maximum). A MESH of an edited column that finishes during the batch is dropped at intake while its column is pinned (`src/engine/coordinator.ts:1263`), so no partial paste is meshed in the pinned columns.
- **Accepted partial views.** The pins cover each edited column's 3 × 3 (`coordinator.ts:1564-1571`); the relight's edge bits can dirty columns one step further out (`edits.ts:371-373`). A MESH of such a column that runs between sub-batches can show the light of the sub-batches already published, until the reply's `markEdited` remeshes it. The main thread's raycast and walking also read the published sub-batches before the reply. Both last at most the batch's `editMs` and end at the reply; this spec accepts them.

### 3.3 The `copy` message and the length cap (D22)

- `ToSim` gains `copy {id, x0, y0, z0, w, h, d, gen}`: `id`, the corner and the sizes integers, 1 ≤ w, h, d ≤ 64, `gen` a boolean; anything else is BAD_MESSAGE (a page bug: the page's sizes come from a checked selection or clipboard).
- **The reply** `copied {id, values: Uint32Array | null, generated: Uint32Array | null, why: 'outside' | 'unloaded' | null}`, the buffers transferred:
  - a box not inside y −64 … 319 and the window: `values` null, `why: 'outside'`, nothing read. A read is harmless, so it is not a fatal guard (an `error` reply latches the handler, `src/workers/simHandler.ts:254-256`);
  - else the sim reads `blockAt << 8 | fluidAt` for each cell in one read section, in order x fastest, then z, then y (index `(y · d + z) · w + x`). Streaming frees columns on the main thread at any moment (`freeColumn`, `src/world/store/store.ts:683-699`), and `blockAt` of a column that is not Published returns −1 (`store.ts:763-765`). So the read stops at the first −1 and answers `values` null, `why: 'unloaded'`; a −1 is never shifted into a value;
  - with `gen`, also the generated value of each cell (`proto`'s block and fluid, as `generatedAt`, `src/sim/edits.ts:244`; the page keeps every proto, `retainAllProtos`, `src/ui/world/worldPage.ts:2832`), or `why: 'unloaded'` when a column's proto is gone.
  Measured cost: about 88 ns per cell, 23 ms for 64³, in the sim; with `gen`, about twice that.
- **The client's guard** (`parseFromSim` and `simClient`, as for `edited`, `src/engine/simClient.ts:15-20`): a `copied` must answer a pending `copy` by id, `why` one of the three, `values` (and `generated` when asked) null or a `Uint32Array` of length w · h · d, and every value's state below `REGISTRY.stateCount` with bit 7 of its fluid byte clear. A reply that fails is the sim's failure, as any malformed reply.
- `parseToSim`'s `edits` guard (`protocol.ts:836`) refuses a batch longer than 32,768 (`MAX_STEP_EDITS`, declared once in `src/sim/edits.ts`: `workers/` may value-import the sim layer but never `ui/`, `test/arch/rules/imports.ts:27`).
- `simClient.copy(box, gen)` resolves with the reply; a sim failure rejects it with `SimFailed`, as `edits` does.

### 3.4 Bulk batches (D23)

`createEditPath.post` (`editPath.ts:78-118`): for a batch of more than 2 edits it skips `latency.posted` (`:103`) and, if the reply has not come 250 ms after the post, shows "Working on n blocks…" until it does. Undo and redo of a large step go through the same `post` (`blockHistory.ts:84-103`) and so are bulk too. `EditPath` gains `bulkPending()`: whether a bulk batch is posted and not answered; the autosave reads it (§2.8). The gate, the pins and the reply handling are unchanged.

### 3.5 Measured costs that set the bounds

From the engine recon's §11 (Node, the reference CPU, single runs about ±15 %, the pool already grown in every case but the one-batch maximum; Chrome's worker expected within about 1.2×, not measured; L12 measures it, §10.4):

| case | changed | sim, one batch | sim, sub-batches of 1,024 | step size (JSON) | remesh |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| house 32 × 12 × 32 | 3,296 | 168-200 ms | 155 ms, ≤ 67 ms per sub-batch | 188 KB | 22-36 ms |
| solid 32 × 32 × 32 | 32,768 | 1,312-1,334 ms (809-881 validate) | 411 ms, ≤ 70 ms per sub-batch | 1,850 KB | 15-26 ms |

A relight-heavy build cost about 45 µs per changed block in the 3,296-change house against about 5 µs for solid blocks. **Estimated, not measured:** 32,768 changes of such a build take about 1.5 s in sub-batches. The estimate is not a bound: a roof voxel's relight re-seeds the sky column's whole fall under it (`src/light/relight.ts:4-5`), and its cost depends on the order the batch writes (recon §11). D31's prototype measures it as `paste.maxLit` (§10.3) before the cap is coded.

The main thread's share at 32,768: the batch's structured clone 5.8 ms. **The DiffStore** grows by about 1 MB of heap, 145 KB on disk and about 7 ms at every open per 32,768 kept changes (recon §10), and it has no cap of its own: ten maximum pastes kept in a world cost about 10 MB of heap, 1.5 MB on disk and about 70 ms at every open of it. No cumulative bound is set, because the same entries can be made by clicks, which SP5b does not bound either, and a bound would refuse building in a large world; `E3.paste` records the entries and the load's decode time after its pastes (§10.1). The history, not the DiffStore, is the binding storage cost (§5).

## 4. The clipboard

### 4.1 `ClipboardV1` (D24)

`{v: 1, w, h, d, keys: string[], states: Uint16Array, fluids: Uint8Array}`: `w`, `h`, `d` integers 1-64; `keys` the distinct state keys used (`REGISTRY.stateKey`, `registry.ts:119`), at most 4,096 (`MAX_STATES`, master §2.2); `states[i]` an index into `keys` and `fluids[i]` the fluid byte of cell `i = (y · d + z) · w + x`; both of length w · h · d. Decode, in `persist/clipboard.ts`, maps each key through `parseStateKey` (`registry.ts:121`) to today's id; a key that does not parse becomes air (state 0, fluid 0) and is counted (D15's notice). A value that fails a check makes the whole clipboard unreadable (D15's "could not be read"), never a throw: `v` not 1, a size out of 1-64, an array of the wrong type or length, an index ≥ `keys.length`, a fluid byte with bit 7 set (`fluid.ts:1-5`), more than 4,096 keys. `persist/clipboard.ts` imports the registry as `saves.ts` does for its remap (`src/persist/saves.ts:336-344`). At most 3 B per cell: 768 KiB at 64³.

### 4.2 Where it lives (D15, D26)

- `wi10`'s `meta` store (string keys, `src/persist/kv.ts:9-14`), key `'clipboard'`, one `put` per copy or cut through the main thread's `KV.write`. No version bump: `VERSION` stays 1 (`idb.ts:20`). In a saved world the page uses its own connection (`saved.kv`, `worldPage.ts:3864`); in an unsaved world it opens `wi10` at the first copy or paste (`openIdbKV`, `idb.ts:158`; `idbAvailable`, `:149`); in the bench, the bench's database (`wi10-bench`); with no storage, memory only (D15's notice once).
- Ctrl+V and nothing else reads it: one `get` of `meta['clipboard']`, then decode; a failed read uses the page's memory copy, if any. So a copy in another tab is pasted here.
- Both connections' transactions span every store (`src/persist/idb.ts:4-6`), so a clipboard read or write runs after the transactions already issued: at most the sim's flush in flight (the sim issues one at a time; a queued one is not yet a transaction). A flush's commit takes about 160 ms in Firefox on the Iris Xe (`docs/backlog.md:36`), more with a history near its cap, so a Ctrl+V or a cut can wait that long; L12 records `clipboardReadMs` and `clipboardWriteMs` (§10.4).
- `meta` is not in a world's Delete (E7's range covers `worlds`, `state`, `history`, `diffs`), its Import, its export (master §2.7's header) or its backup copy (`copyOps`, `saves.ts:545-558`); a unit test checks that `meta['clipboard']` survives each (§10.5). The KV contract suite uses `meta` in its own database (`src/persist/kvContract.ts:136-152`), never `wi10`.

## 5. Undo, redo and the history's caps (D14)

- A cut, paste or move is one `BlockStep` (`blockHistory.ts:38-40`), recorded by `createActionPost` from the reply's `prev` and `next`; undo posts it reversed, redo in order (`editsOf`, `:83-97`), through the sub-batched path, the same cap, the same `'changed'` drop (SP5b D32).
- `MAX_STEP_EDITS = 32,768` is declared once, in `src/sim/edits.ts` (the sim layer, which `workers/`, `persist/` and `ui/` may all value-import; the sim's guard in `workers/` cannot import `ui/`), and imported by `protocol.ts`, `blockHistory.ts` and `saves.ts`. `MAX_HISTORY_EDITS = 65,536` is declared in `blockHistory.ts` and mirrored in `saves.ts` beside `HISTORY_STEPS` (`saves.ts:125`; `persist/` cannot import `ui/`), pinned equal by `test/unit/stateBounds.test.ts` (as `:29` pins the step count). `record` drops the oldest undo steps until the total edits of both sides are ≤ 65,536 (the new step is kept whole; recording clears the redo side, so only undo steps are dropped). `restore` and the normaliser (`normalizeStep`, `normalizeHistory`, `saves.ts:346-383`) drop a step over 32,768 edits and then, while the total is over 65,536, the oldest undo step, then the redo step furthest from the present.
- Why a total: the sim writes the `history` record whole whenever it changed (`flushOps`, `saves.ts:539`), so every later click, undo or redo rewrites every kept step: at 65,536 edits about 2.9 MB and about 9 ms of clone in the sim per history-changing flush (recon §7, §12). The flush message usually carries only new steps (`historyChange`, `src/workers/protocol.ts:681-686`), so a new 32,768-edit step costs the main thread one clone of about 4.7 ms in one flush. L12 records both (§10.4).
- **Where the whole history still crosses the main thread** (at the cap, about 2.9 MB and 65,536 edits):
  - at open, the page reads the `history` record and normalises it edit by edit (`src/ui/world/worldPage.ts:1949`; `normalizeStep`, `src/persist/saves.ts:346-358`), then restores it;
  - the first history-changing flush after every open posts the whole history, because a `load` clears the client's change base (`src/engine/simClient.ts:9-11`);
  - every `backup` carries the whole history (`protocol.ts:707-716`), and the backup copy writes it under the new id;
  - an export writes it in the `.wiworld` header as canonical JSON (master §2.7), about 2.9 MB at the cap.
  None of these is measured at the cap. `E3.paste` records, in Node, the open's normalise-and-restore time and the first flush's clone with a 65,536-edit history (§10.1). If either is above 16 ms on the reference CPU, D28's lever 3 (per-step history records) moves before the exit.
- **Master §4.12's Block undo line** ("the last 100 undo and 100 redo steps per world") is kept as written: the 100-step bound still holds, and the new total is stated in §13's new Selection tool paragraph. A reader of the Block undo line alone would miss the total; the paragraph sits right after it.

### 6.2 The ghost (D11, D25)

- `render/ghost.ts` builds from the clipboard's values (unturned): for each 16³ cell, a `PaddedSection` (`src/world/store/padded.ts:26-46`) filled from the clipboard (its border from the neighbouring cells, air outside; light `0xF0`, sky 15, as above the world, `padded.ts:23-24`), `meshSolid` and `meshBoxes` (`src/mesh/greedy.ts:145`, `src/mesh/boxes.ts:59`) with AO on, appended into one `BufferGeometry` whose positions are widened to Uint16 in 1/8-block units as RegionMesh's (`src/render/regionMesh.ts:11-13`). A cell is the unit of work: a frame's build stops after the cell that crosses 1 ms (one 16³ cell cannot be split), and L12 gates the longest slice (§10.4). The geometry is uploaded once when the last cell is done. Fluids are not meshed. Over 131,072 quads the build stops and the ghost shows its box only (D11). The mesher writes opaque and cutout quads as separate passes (`src/mesh/greedy.ts:141-145`); the ghost appends both into its one geometry, so its material must discard transparent texels.
- The material: `MaterialSystem` (`src/render/materials/index.ts`, whose comment reserves "selection" for its SP, `:14`) gains `ghost`, the terrain shader (`src/render/materials/glsl/terrain.glsl.ts`) with `CUTOUT` and a new `GHOST` define, `transparent: true`, `depthWrite: false`, depth-tested, drawn after the water. Under `GHOST` the fragment:
  - writes alpha from a uniform, 0.45 (today it writes 1.0, `terrain.glsl.ts:98-99`);
  - remaps the face id by a `ghostTurns` uniform (0-3) before both its uses, the face shade (`FACE_SHADE`, `:16-26`, `:98`) and glass's in-face coordinates (`:92-96`): north → east → south → west for each clockwise turn, up and down unchanged. Without it, a turned east face keeps east's 0.6 while facing north, and a turned glass face tests a coordinate that is constant across it, so the pane never discards and shows solid;
  - takes its light as `max(lightmapAt(vBlock, vSky), vec3(0.6))`: the ghost is meshed with sky 15 (open sky), which the lightmap dims at night, and the floor keeps it visible then.
  It is compiled behind the loading screen with the outline (`compile`, `src/render/renderer.ts:465-470`), so the first Ctrl+V does not stall on a program link.
- Placing: the mesh's position is the box's minimum corner relative to the floating origin (`worldPage.ts:1770-1776`'s pattern) plus the turn's translation, and its rotation about y is −q · 90°: q = 1 translates by (D, 0, 0), q = 2 by (W, 0, D), q = 3 by (0, 0, W) (W and D the unturned sizes), which maps a cell as §2.6 does. Moving the ghost writes its matrix only; turning it writes its matrix and `ghostTurns`.

## 8. Failure modes and review focus

- **A paste half applied** (a `StoreFull` mid-batch): §3.2's headroom check makes it rare; the compensating pass, in sub-batches with drains, handles it; a unit test with a really full store (§10.5), not only an injected throw.
- **The sim killed by a malformed paste or read** (a cell outside y or the window): §2.7's checks before posting an edit or a target `copy`; a `copy` outside answers `why: 'outside'`, not BAD_MESSAGE (§3.3); the guard's length cap.
- **The quadratic validation returning** (a refactor dropping D20): `paste.max`'s gate (§10.3) and L12.
- **Pool growth returning** (a refactor dropping the sub-batches or their `exitRead`; `paste.max` would still pass, at about 450-520 ms): `L3.paste`'s `pasteLimboOver` (§10.1); the pools' byte lengths before and after recorded in `L3.paste` and L12c.
- **A paste lost by leaving at once:** the leave waits for a pending bulk batch (§2.8); the `leaveAfterSave` unit test and `E3.paste`'s leave case.
- **A ghost read past the world's edge:** the main-thread checks at each ghost position post no `copy` (§2.5); `selectTool`'s test.
- **A column freed during a read:** the read stops at the first −1 and answers `unloaded` (§3.3); `simEdits.test.ts`.
- **A cut lost on reload:** the cut waits for the clipboard's commit (§2.2); `E3.paste`'s cut-then-crash case (§10.1).
- **An undo that does not restore a whole volume:** one step per operation, undo through the same path; `E3.paste`.
- **Light wrong after a paste** (sub-batches publishing partial light): `L3.paste` against a fresh `lightRegion`.
- **A rotated block facing the wrong way:** §2.6's single rule, the property tests and the named wall-torch case (§10.5).
- **A clipboard read under a different registry:** keys, not ids (§4.1); `E3.paste`'s cross-world case.
- **The history rewritten huge at every click:** the 65,536 total (§5); L12's `flushSyncMaxMs`.
- **A stutter while placing** (the ghost's build, the target reads): the build slices of one cell, the target read only after 100 ms still and one in flight, the reads in the sim; L12's sweep part `sweepOver33` and `ghostSliceMaxMs` (§10.4).
- **Streaming and clicks stalled behind a paste:** the sim is busy for the whole batch, about 0.4-1.5 s at the maximum (§3.2), and a D job's diff read-through (master §4.13), a flush, a target `copy` and every click wait for it; L12's `diffWaitMaxMs` (recorded; expected at most the batch's `editMs` plus one sub-batch).
- **A ghost that misleads after a turn** (face shading or glass wrong): the `ghostTurns` remap (§6.2); a turned glass pane in `review-rotated-paste.png` (§10.10).
- **A held RMB pasting repeatedly:** the tool bypasses the hold repeat (§1.2).
- **Ctrl+C in a map text field copying blocks:** the key resolves only with the world owning the controls and no text focus (§1.1).
- **The partial paste meshed:** the pinned-column drop (§3.2).
- **CI's wall growing:** §11.
