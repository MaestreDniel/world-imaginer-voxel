## Goal

Water now lives where the terrain puts it. On mountains, here and there a spring flows out of a rock face and falls as a waterfall to the ground below, where it spreads and stops; on floating islands, springs pour from the islands' edges to the ground far below. The waterfalls never run dry: the spring is a permanent source. Seas, rivers and lakes fill at their own levels, and where the coast or a river bank leaves a step, the water flows down it instead of standing as a wall. Underground, caves that open under the sea or a river are flooded, with cracks in the sea floor leading into them, while caves under dry land below sea level are mostly dry, kept dry by rock barriers; deeper down, some caves hold lakes of water or lava, and the deepest gullies end in lava. The abyss holds no water at all: it is lit only by lava, falling from springs in its walls and pooling on its floor. Flowing water and lava show their slope, their falls and the steps between levels. At Medium, water is Fancy: it reflects the sky at grazing angles and ripples. Under water the view closes into a blue fog; in lava, into a short red one. Lush cave floors under water are clay, other cave floors under water gravel. The X-ray tints flooded and lava-filled caves, and the slice view shows aquifer water in caves. Underneath: an aquifer of 16 × 12 × 16 cells with barriers and lava, evaluated per voxel in T; springs placed by T; an exact settle of falls and spreads in D over the 3 × 3 columns, capped at 12 blocks of horizontal travel for water and 14 for lava; a mesher for levels, corner heights and side strips; a water tier uniform; an underwater fog. Measured by A1-A5 (A5 new: springs and falls), A2 after settle, B2's voxel part, B5 with its third part, R5, DT1 with settle, P1 and the Chrome legs. The generated world changes: `GENERATOR_VERSION` 9 → 10 at the one push that changes generated output (D33). The runtime fluid simulation ("break a lake rim and watch it drain") is SP7b.

## Decisions taken by this spec (for the user's review)

### Decisions with a visible effect

- **D1 Waterfalls from springs in mountain faces** (§3.2). *You notice:* on steep ground, a stream comes out of a rock face one block wide, falls straight down at least 8 blocks onto the ground below and spreads there into a small pool-shaped spill that thins out over about 7 blocks; it never runs dry. The spring sits in natural rock (stone, deepslate, dirt, calcite, terracotta and the like; never sand or gravel), with rock above, below and on three sides and its one open side facing the drop, as Minecraft's spring. Springs appear only where a face drops at least 8 blocks below them and is open to the sky, so they land on mountains and cliffs, not on flat land or in caves. Alternative: Minecraft's uniform tries at any height (about 25 per chunk, **MC (memory)**), which put most springs in cave walls and few on mountains.
- **D2 How many waterfalls** (Point 2). *You notice:* with the user's choice (about 50 per km²), about one waterfall per 140 × 140 blocks of steep land, about 6 in view in all-mountain country at RD 12; positions look scattered (no rows, no spacing pattern; `A5.clarkEvans` and `A5.borderRatio`, §13.1). Alternative: Point 2's (a), (b) or (c).
- **D3 Island-edge waterfalls** (§3.3; agreed). *You notice:* in floating_islands, now and then a spring pours from an island's side and falls to the ground or sea below, a long thin waterfall; about 5 per km² of island area (**estimate**, tuned at D31). Alternative: none (SP8b, as the master had it).
- **D4 The abyss: lava only** (§1.6, §3.4). *You notice:* the abyss never holds water: no aquifer water there, and a stream that falls into it from above stops at its edge (rare: it needs a spring stream to find a shaft into the abyss). Lava comes from three places: lava springs in the abyss's walls (lava falls of at least 4 blocks that spread about 3 blocks where they land), lava pools from aquifer lava cells where the abyss lies below y −10, and the gullies of the rock floor (D7). Each lava voxel lights its surroundings (D14), so the abyss is dark except near lava. Alternative: SP6b's other suggestion, a lava mask lowering the rock floor locally so wide lava lakes fill the abyss's bottom (more lava, moves C7's bottom parts, D7's alternative).
- **D5 Caves open under the sea and rivers; lakes stay sealed** (§2; agreed for sea and rivers, Point 3 for lakes). *You notice:* the sea floor and river beds have cracks and holes into caves, which are full of water; flooded caves under the sea are common, as in Minecraft, because cells under the ocean flood more readily (§1.3); elevated lakes keep a sealed bed (no cave within 20 blocks below them). Alternative: Point 3's.
- **D6 Dry caves below sea level** (§1). *You notice:* under land, caves below y 63 are mostly dry; here and there a cave holds a lake at its own level (a partially flooded cell, levels at varied heights); where a flooded and a dry part meet, a rock wall (a barrier) usually separates them; where none does, water spills out and falls into the dry part (an underground waterfall: the master's "waterfalls at aquifer borders"). A1 holds ≥ 40 % of such cave air dry. Alternative: Minecraft's exact thresholds without the 2D `offset` inputs (D18), which needs the 3D surface estimate.
- **D7 Deep lava** (§1.4). *You notice:* below y −10, some cave lakes are lava instead of water (aquifer lava cells, levels from about −29 to −11, and from −55 to −51 in the deepest cells); and the lowest voxel of the rock floor's gullies (y −55) holds lava wherever no cell claims it (master :756's "everything below y −54 is lava", kept as written, a 1-block lava puddle at the bottom of the deepest gullies). No floor change: SP6b's rock floor, its mounds and gullies stay, and no bedrock shows. Alternative: D4's lava mask lowering the floor, or a raised global lava line (e.g. −50), which fills 4-5 blocks of every gully with a flat lava plane at one height (reads as a flat floor again, the thing the user disliked at the world's bottom in SP6a).
- **D8 Seas, rivers and lakes at their own level, and their walls flow** (§1.7, §4). *You notice:* seas and rivers at 63, lakes at their level, as today; where water v0 left a wall of water against air (a river bank whose position is dry while the next is wet, an archipelago's sea above a lake below sea level, master :1955; the SP3e spec :190), the water now flows down over it as a short spill. Mountain gorges stay dry unless a spring feeds them. Alternative: also raise rivers to their own height in gorges (§17, a proposal for SP12), which changes the column stage and the map.
- **D9 Flowing water and lava look** (§6). *You notice:* a spreading spill slopes down from its source, each block a ninth lower; where two levels meet, the step shows a thin side strip instead of a hole; a falling stream is a full-height column with straight sides; lava flows the same way (shorter, 3 blocks); still lava's surface now sits at 8/9 of a block like water's (it was a full block). Alternative: lava sources at full height (SP5a's look), which leaves a 1/9 step where still lava meets flowing lava.
- **D10 Fancy water at Medium** (§7). *You notice:* water reflects the sky more at grazing angles (Fresnel) and shows two layers of small moving ripples; the deep-to-shallow colour stays. Settings › Graphics gains "Water: Fast / Fancy", live, Fancy by default (master §4.16: Medium is Fancy, :1261). Alternative: Fast stays the default until SP11's quality presets (Point 1 (a)).
- **D11 Underwater and in lava** (§8). *You notice:* when the eye is below the water's surface, the view closes into a blue fog (fully fogged at about 56 blocks, denser in deep or dark water) with a blue tint, the sky seen through the surface included; in lava, an orange-red fog fully closed at about 3 blocks. Leaving the water returns the normal fog in the same frame. The X-ray view is unaffected. Alternative: fog only (no tint), which leaves the sky dome untinted under water.

## 1. Aquifers (D5-D7, D17-D20; master §3.9)

### 1.1 Cells

As master :748: cells 16 × 12 × 16 blocks with the grid offset (5, 1, 5); each cell's centre jittered by (0-9, 0-8, 0-9) from h = `hash3(deriveSeed(seed, 'aquifer.cell'), i, j, k) >>> 0` (the cell's indices; `hash4` is a salted 2D hash, `src/core/hash.ts:36`, so the jitter is `hash3`, `:32`, as the cave biome's), x + h mod 10, y + (h >>> 8) mod 9, z + (h >>> 16) mod 10 from the cell's corner (16i, 12j, 16k); each voxel takes the 4 nearest centres among the 2 × 3 × 2 neighbourhood (squared distances, ties to the lower cell index; master :748's 4, where Minecraft keeps 3, **MC (memory)**; only the two nearest decide here, so the count is harmless). A cell's status is (level, fluid type): level an integer or −∞ (empty), type water or lava.

### 1.2 Levels near the surface (D18)

For a cell with grid column (gx, gz) (its un-jittered x, z) and centre y: `p = samplePoint(gx, gz)`. If `centre.y ≥ p.offset − aquifer.surfaceBand` (12), the cell takes ⌊`p.surfaceWaterLevel`⌋: 63 under sea or river, Lw under a lake, −∞ on land. So near the surface, caves under the sea and rivers flood to 63, caves under land stay empty, and no near-surface cell floods dry land (a reading of master :751 that also covers the land case).

### 1.3 Floodedness and partial levels

Otherwise, Nf = `aquifer.noises.floodedness` (3D) at the centre, clamped to [−1, 1]; the ocean factor t = clamp((63 − p.offset)/8, 0, 1); thresholds lerp(inland, ocean, t): **fully flooded** if Nf > lerp(0.8, −0.3, t) (level 63, water); **partially flooded** if Nf > lerp(0.4, −0.8, t), level = ⌊centre.y/40⌋·40 + 20 + 3·round(Nspread·10/3) (Nspread = `aquifer.noises.spread` at the centre, clamped to [−1, 1]; levels −9 … +9 around each 40-block band's middle); otherwise **empty** (master :752-755). Under the sea (t = 1) most deep cells flood; inland few do. The thresholds are leaves (§1.9).

### 1.4 Lava (D7)

A partially flooded cell whose level ≤ `aquifer.lavaMaxLevel` (−10) and |Nlava| > `aquifer.lavaThreshold` (0.3), Nlava = `aquifer.noises.lava` sampled once per 64 × 40 × 64 block (at the block's corner), is lava (master :756). Below the master's line, any tagged non-solid voxel at y < `aquifer.lavaLevel` (−54) that no cell fills is lava (a source; master's "everything below y −54 is lava", kept): under SP6b's rock floor no cave air lies below −55 (the SP6b spec §5.2), so this fills only y −55 in the deepest gullies. Expected lava cave lakes: levels about −29 … −11 (cells centred −40 … 0) and −55 … −51 (cells centred below −40). **Why not the floor mask** (SP6b's hand-over, master :1956): it moves `C7.bottomPlane`, `bottomFloorSpread`, `bottomFlatRatio` and `bedrockExposed` and adds a noise and a leaf, for a lava plane at the world's bottom that this spec does not need: A3's first part (lava below −54 in ≥ 50 % of 32 × 32-chunk regions) is met by the −55 puddles and the deep lava cells, measured at D31 (lever: `aquifer.lavaLevel` −54 → −52, then the floor mask as the user's choice). `C7.bedrockExposed` is re-checked on every group (its population, cave air with no fluid, never touches lava's bedrock neighbours: the rock floor keeps air ≥ −55 and bedrock ≤ −60).

### 1.5 Barriers (D20)

At a tagged non-solid voxel (D17), when its two nearest centres have different statuses and d2 − d1 < 25 (squared-distance units, master :758), Minecraft's pressure formula (**MC (memory)**: a similarity 1 − (d2 − d1)/25 times a pressure from the two levels and the voxel's height, plus `aquifer.noises.barrier` × 2) decides; a positive result places a barrier: the voxel becomes solid, its tag kind and nibble 0, its state chosen by the surface pass (stone, deepslate, rule 4's skin or the abyss's strata). Otherwise the voxel takes the nearest centre's status: fluid if y ≤ level, else air, for water and lava alike: this project's levels are the highest fluid y (water v0's `y ≤ waterTop`, D19's sky-open rule `y ≤ ⌊surfaceWaterLevel⌋`), and Minecraft's `at(y)` (fluid at y < level) is the same rule with its level one higher (Spec correction 7; otherwise a crack under the sea would hold a dry layer at y 63 between the cave water and the sea). Two statuses hold fluid at y, for the pressure's water-against-lava case, by the same rule.

### 1.6 The abyss (D4)

Two tests per tagged voxel, both point functions:
- **Inside the abyss:** the voxel's own cave nibble (written before the aquifer, D17) is the abyss (3). The nibble is picked at a position jittered by up to `caves.biomes.edgeJitter` (2) blocks in x, y and z (`caveBiomeOfPass`, `src/gen/biomes/cavePicker.ts:183-186`; `src/core/params/schema.ts:365`), so it is tested directly, not through the unjittered box. There a water status yields **air**: the aquifer never writes water to an abyss voxel. Lava statuses and the −54 rule apply unchanged.
- **The margin:** a voxel whose nibble is not the abyss but which lies inside the abyss box widened on every side, from the cave-biome column pass's unjittered C′, E′ and Y0 (`cavePicker.ts:120-151`; the point function reads them through `caveBiomeUnjittered`, `:189-191`): E′ ≤ `abyss.eMax` + m, C′ > `abyss.cMin` − m and depth > `abyss.depthMin` − 0.1 (master :760's 0.1 depth margin, about 13 blocks at `caves.biomes.depthScale` 128), with m = `aquifer.abyssMargin` in C′ and E′ units. There a water status yields **barrier rock** where it would place water (y at or below the level) and air elsewhere, so water never touches abyss air from above or from the side.

## 3. Springs (D1-D4, D21)

### 3.1 The common rule

A spring is a source voxel placed in a solid voxel of the proto: the voxel becomes AIR plus `WATER_SOURCE` or `LAVA_SOURCE` (master :334). Per chunk (cx, cz) and kind k, `Xoshiro128(hash2(deriveSeed(seed, 'springs.' + k), cx, cz))` draws `springs.k.tries` tries in a fixed order, each (x, z) uniform in the chunk, a direction d of the four horizontal ones and u ∈ [0, 1); the try picks a height y from its kind's range (below) and is accepted when:
- the spring voxel is solid with a state in the kind's valid set (water: every `CARVABLE` terrain state except sand, red_sand and gravel; lava: deepslate and the five abyss rocks), not a spring already, and not tagged;
- the voxels above and below it and the two lateral and one back neighbours are solid (`solidAt`);
- the outlet (the neighbour in d) is non-solid with no fluid, and the `minFall` voxels below the outlet are non-solid with no fluid.
Reads inside the column use T's arrays as they stand before any spring is written; reads outside it use the point functions (`solidAt`, `fluidAt`, `caveTagAt`, `caveBiomeAt`), equal to bulk (DT2) and blind to springs too, so the decision is identical from any column and no border ring is excluded. Only tries near a border need probes (about 1 in 8, ≤ `minFall` + 4 probes each). Accepted springs are written after the surface pass (D17), so the voxels around a spring keep the skin of a solid run. Because no try sees another spring, two springs can end up side by side (one in the other's lateral or back face), which makes each two-faced; this is accepted as rare (**estimate**: under 1 per 10 km²) and counted (`A5.adjacentSprings`, recorded), rather than having every border try replay the neighbour's tries.

### 3.2 Water springs in mountain faces (D1, D2)

Range: yLo = ⌈`offset` at the outlet⌉ + `springs.water.minFall` (8), the outlet's 2D surface estimate read by `readFieldHalo` (the SP6b spec §1.2; exact for positions within 4 blocks of the column); yHi = `top` − 2 (with `islands.enabled`, `groundTop` − 2, §1.7, so island faces are left to §3.3); y = yLo + ⌊u·(yHi − yLo + 1)⌋, rejected if yHi < yLo or y < `springs.water.minY` (64). With the outlet above its column's surface estimate by ≥ 8, the fall is open to the sky in practice; A5 measures the fall on the settled world and counts a waterfall as **visible** when the outlet has no solid voxel above it up to y 319 and its straight fall is ≥ 8 (§13.1). Default `springs.water.tries` 6 (**estimate** for Point 2 (b); D31 tunes it on `A5.waterfalls`).

### 3.3 Island-edge springs (D3)

With `islands.enabled`: the range is an island run of the column (a solid run whose bottom ≥ 181, SP3e's runs): the run is the k-th island run where k = ⌊u·runs⌋, and y uniform in [bottom + 1, top − 1] of that run from a second draw; outlet and fall as §3.1 with `springs.island.minFall` (8). The fall below an island's edge reaches the ground or the sea (often 100-200 blocks; falling is unlimited, master :924). Default `springs.island.tries` 4 (**estimate**).

### 3.4 Lava springs in the abyss (D4)

Range: yLo = −55 + `springs.lava.minFall` (4), yHi = min(`top` − 2, ⌊Y0(x, z) − `caves.biomes.depthScale`·`caves.biomes.abyss.depthMin`⌋) (the abyss's top at that position, the picker's own inputs); the outlet must be tagged cave air whose nibble is the abyss (3) with no fluid. The fall then spreads about 3 blocks where it lands (§4.1). Default `springs.lava.tries` 16 (**estimate**; abyss air is a small share of each column's height, so many tries miss).

### 3.5 Parameters and cost

A new `springs` group, Terrain scope: `springs.water.{tries 3, minY 64, minFall 8}` (3 is the starting **estimate** for Point 2's 50 per km², half the 6 first sized for 100; D31 tunes it), `springs.island.{tries 4, minFall 8}`, `springs.lava.{tries 16, minFall 4}`: 7 leaves. Terrain scope means an edit re-runs T (the decorate-scope Apply path stays SP8b's, master :1973). Cost **estimate**: 26 tries per chunk, a few array reads each and about 3 tries per chunk with probes: < 0.05 ms per column.

## 4. The gen-time settle (D22, D23; master §3.14)

### 4.1 Rules (`fluidRules.ts`)

Fluid states use the frozen byte (`src/world/blocks/fluid.ts:1-27`): level 0 source … 7, bit 3 falling. For a fluid f (water: levels 0 … 7, +1 per block, search 4; lava: levels 0, 2, 4, 6, +2 per block, so 3 blocks of reach, search 2, Minecraft's overworld lava, **MC (memory)**; the byte stores the level as Minecraft writes it, so both fluids share §6.1's heights):
- **Two fixed points.** Water and lava settle independently: each fluid's settle treats every voxel holding the other type's proto fluid as `block`. The two results are then merged per voxel: a voxel reached by both takes lava with `unsettled` = 1 (a contact, which SP7b's reactions resolve), and every other voxel keeps its own type's result. The merge reads one voxel of each result, so it is local and order-free. (Neither type reads the other's settled result, which would make the first front processed win a contested voxel: the fixed point would not be unique, and two receivers could resolve one contact differently.)
- **Falling:** a fluid voxel whose voxel below is passable (non-solid or `hold`/`displace`) makes it falling f (falling bit, level 0), unless the proto holds a source of f there; falls are unlimited.
- **Spreading:** a source spreads sideways always; a flowing or falling voxel spreads sideways only when its voxel below is not passable or holds f **in the proto** (a stream reaching a lake or the sea spreads over it, as in Minecraft; a stream reaching water the settle itself placed falls into it, which makes that voxel falling, as Minecraft's falling water does). The spread goes to the open sides at minimal distance to the nearest **drop** (a passable voxel with a passable voxel below) within the search reach, by a breadth-first search over passable voxels; with no drop in reach, to every open side. A voxel reached sideways from level k carries k + the fluid's step (a landed fall spreads as a source would), up to level 7 (water) or 6 (lava).
- **New sources:** a passable voxel with ≥ 2 horizontal water sources **of the proto** beside it, over a solid voxel or a proto water source, becomes a water source (water only). Minecraft's rule reads any source; the settle makes no source except by this rule, so reading the proto only drops the cascade of rule-made sources across a flat floor, which is what would make the reach unbounded.
- **Order:** a voxel's settled value is the least any feeder gives it, in the order source < falling < flowing 1 < … < 7 < dry. Every condition that reads fluid reads the proto, and the passability, drop and nibble tests read blocks, so each rule can only lower a value when its feeders' values fall: the rules are monotone, and the settled world is their least fixed point from the proto, unique whatever the processing order.
- **Distance:** every fluid voxel carries a horizontal distance per value: 0 for a proto source's own value, and for each value k the least, over the derivations that give the voxel k, of the feeder's distance at the value it spreads + 1 for a sideways step (+0 for a fall). A derivation of k is kept only when its distance is below the voxel's distance at every value ≤ k (a lower value no farther from its source already does everything it would), so the per-value distances only fall and the rules stay monotone. The voxel's value is its least (Order) and its distance is that value's, so its level and its distance come from one derivation, and the cap reads the distance of the level that spreads (a voxel fed at level 2 from 9 blocks along a corridor, with a fall on the way, and at level 3 from 3 blocks across has level 2 and distance 9, and its level-3 derivation still spreads level 4 from distance 3; without a fall a water level is at most its distance, since both start at 0 and rise by 1 per sideways step: the plan's Spec correction 13). Taking the distance independently of the level would let a level travel along one feeder's path while the cap counts along another's, past the radius below. A source made by the new-source rule takes the least of its feeders' distances + 1, so the count never restarts. A voxel at the cap, **12** (water) or **14** (lava), does not spread sideways; it keeps `unsettled` = 1 when its spread rule names a passable target (a test on blocks only, so it reads 1 block; the target may already hold fluid, in which case the flag is a harmless wake-up for SP7b; master :931).
- **Radius:** every read that decides a voxel v lies on or beside a feeding path from a proto source to v: the passability and proto fluid of the path's voxels and of the voxels below them, the nearest-drop search up to 4 (2) blocks beyond each path voxel, and the new-source rule's proto reads 1 block beyond. The distance of the voxel's value grows by one per sideways step along the path that derived it, so each path voxel lies within the cap of v horizontally, and every read lies within cap + search = 12 + 4 = 16 (water) or 14 + 2 = 16 (lava) blocks of v (the new-source rule's 1 block and the `unsettled` test's 1 block are within the search's reach). K's 3 × 3 extends 16 blocks beyond K on every side, so every derivation of a voxel of K reads only the window, and the windowed result on K equals a global settle. This is the proof; §13.4's `DT1.settleReference` measures it.
- **Never:** a water front never enters a voxel whose cave nibble is the abyss (proto data); that front ends there without `unsettled` (a rule, not a truncation; `A5.waterIntoAbyss` counts them and `C7.waterWall` counts them as walls, §13.2).
The same functions are SP7b's sim's single-step rules (its tick applies them to a scheduled voxel); SP7a unit-tests the converged shapes (F1's diamond of radius 7, falls, infinite sources, the cap).

## 6. The flowing mesher (D9, D26, D27)

### 6.1 Heights

A fluid voxel's height h (one function in `src/world/blocks/fluidRules.ts`, shared by the mesher, the frame step's eye test, D29, and the walk, §9.2): 1 if the same fluid is directly above or it is falling; else (8 − level)/9 (a source 8/9; lava's levels 0, 2, 4, 6 give 8/9, 6/9, 4/9, 2/9). A top corner's height (**MC (memory)**, Minecraft's average): over the up to 4 voxels around the corner, if any holds the same fluid with the same fluid above, 1; else the weighted mean of their heights, a voxel with h ≥ 0.8 weighing 10 and any other fluid voxel of that type weighing 1, a passable fluid-free voxel counting height 0 with weight 1, a solid voxel not counted. `cornerDrop` = round((1 − h)·9), clamped to 0 … 8 (the unit stays ninths, master :1069; a source surface stays drop 1). Each corner's value is a function of its 4 voxels only, so neighbouring quads share their corners exactly (no cracks).

### 6.2 Faces

- **Top:** drawn when the same fluid is not above. Four corner drops; flat quads (four equal drops) merge greedily as today; sloped quads are one per voxel. The split diagonal is the one joining the two corners with the lower summed height (a fixed rule from the corner values, so neighbours agree).
- **Sides against non-fluid, non-occluding neighbours:** from the voxel's bottom to its two top corners' heights, as today (`src/mesh/fluid.ts:139-156`).
- **Sides between two voxels of the same fluid** (new; amends master :1060's "only against non-fluid neighbours", and `:146`'s `nt === ft` skip): when the neighbour's top corners on the shared edge are lower than this voxel's, the strip between them: bottom vertices at the neighbour's corner drops, top vertices at this voxel's, both encoded as `cornerDrop` from the voxel's top (positions at y + 1). R1's brute force (`test/harness/refs.ts:125-126`, `:155`) adds the same rule.
- **Falling:** full height; sides full.
- **Water beside lava** (new): today water skips its side faces against lava because "lava's opaque full-height face covers every plane the two share" (`src/mesh/fluid.ts:7-8`, `:154-156`). With lava sources at 8/9 and flowing lava down to 2/9 (D9), lava's side reaches only its corner heights, which would leave a see-through band. So water draws a side strip against lava from lava's corner heights on the shared edge up to its own, where its own are higher (the same form as the strips between one fluid); lava draws its sides against water from its bottom to its corner heights, as against any non-lava neighbour. Water's up face under lava stays as today. R1's brute force (`test/harness/refs.ts:155`, which already counts water faces against lava as visible) adds the strip rule in lockstep.
- **Depth:** `fluidDepths` (`:196-260`) unchanged (the vertical run, clamped to 15); a falling column joins the pool below as today.

### The abyss margin (Task 6)

§1.6's m, measured on seed '42''s default profile (2026-11-18, a scratch script, not committed). Columns drawn uniformly from cx, cz in −4,000 … 3,999 (a fixed `Xoshiro128` stream) until 4,098 positions were found on the abyss box's unjittered sides, at most 4 per column (91,728 columns drawn, 11,793 of them holding a position inside the box): positions (x, z) of the column's 16 × 16 where, between (x, z) and (x + 1, z) or (x, z + 1), E′ crosses `abyss.eMax` with C′ > `cMin` (4,077 positions) or C′ crosses `cMin` with E′ ≤ `eMax` (21), and the box's depth is reached above the rock floor, (Y0 + 47)/`depthScale` ≥ `depthMin` (SP6b's `BIOME_SCAN_ROOM_Y` −47). The gradient across the side is |∇E′| (or |∇C′|) per block by central differences over the cave-biome column pass. Its 50th percentile 0.00388, **95th 0.00956**, 99th 0.01263, maximum 0.01746 (95th on E′ sides 0.00949, on C′ sides 0.01730). m = 6 × 0.00956 = 0.0573, rounded up to 0.005: **m = 0.06**, a lateral margin of 6.28 blocks at the 95th percentile.
